# Communication and the cost of living far away

Transport determines whether a person can leave home.

Communication helps determine whether they can remain socially connected after leaving.

The historical question is:

> **When did living far from family become cheap enough — informationally and emotionally — to become ordinary?**

## Distance has several costs

Moving away creates more than travel expense.

It also creates:

- difficulty finding jobs before departure
- uncertainty about conditions at destination
- inability to coordinate emergencies
- slower news from parents, spouses and children
- higher cost of maintaining friendships
- weaker ability to send instructions or make plans
- fear of being unreachable

A cheap train ticket does not eliminate these costs.

## Letters make migration possible, but slowly

Postal systems allow long-distance family relations, but communication remains delayed.

A migrant writing home may have to wait days or weeks for a reply.

This affects ordinary decisions:

- illness at home
- money requests
- births and deaths
- marriage discussions
- whether to return
- timing of remittances

See also:

- [`post-telegraph-and-information-delay.md`](post-telegraph-and-information-delay.md)

## Public and household telephones reduce uncertainty

A telephone allows questions to be resolved before someone physically travels.

But the life effect depends on access:

- telephone in the house
- telephone in the village
- public call office
- employer telephone
- neighbor's telephone
- long-distance tariff

"The telephone existed" is therefore not enough.

## Rural China in the 1990s: telecommunications could increase migration

Research using China's National Fixed Point Survey and variation in the installation of village landline phones during 1993 and 1995–2000 finds that access to landline telephones increased the out-migrant worker ratio by roughly **2 percentage points**, about **51% of the sample mean**.

The authors identify two mechanisms:

1. better access to information about outside jobs;
2. easier timely contact with family members left behind.

Source:

- "Telecommunication externality on migration: Evidence from Chinese villages," *China Economic Review* 39 (2016): https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1043951X16300372

This is exactly the kind of result this repository needs.

Communication is not merely a convenience added *after* migration.

It can help make migration itself feasible.

## The psychological cost of migration matters

Migration decisions are partly about whether family separation can be maintained.

The telecommunications study explicitly argues that easier contact with left-behind family reduces the psychological cost of migration.

Later research on rural-to-urban migrants likewise emphasizes that migrants maintain "trans-local" emotional and economic ties to home, while inexpensive mobile communication makes these ties easier to sustain.

Source:

- "Trans-local ties, local ties and psychological well-being among rural-to-urban migrants in Shanghai": https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0277953612002596

Thus mobile communication can change what "leaving home" means.

A migrant can be physically absent while remaining part of daily household decision-making.

## Information changes the geography of job search

Without rapid communication, job search tends to depend heavily on:

- prior migration chains
- relatives
- village contacts
- labor brokers
- traveling first and searching afterward

Telephones allow:

- checking whether work exists before departure
- coordinating arrival
- comparing opportunities
- asking former migrants for information
- changing destinations more quickly

The opportunity map becomes larger because information can travel before the worker does.

## Mobile phones change return urgency

A family emergency under letter-based communication may require someone to return because information is incomplete.

A phone can allow:

- diagnosis of how urgent the problem is
- transfer of money without immediate return
- coordination with siblings
- repeated emotional contact
- postponement or adjustment of travel

This can extend the amount of time a migrant can remain away.

## Communication can preserve family dependence rather than dissolve it

Greater geographic mobility does not necessarily mean greater family independence.

Cheap communication can allow migrants to remain deeply involved in:

- parents' finances
- children's schooling
- farming decisions
- house construction
- weddings
- elder care

Thus technology can simultaneously increase individual mobility and maintain intergenerational household integration.

This is a useful warning against assuming:

> migration → individualization.

Sometimes better communication makes **distributed family life** possible.

## Questions for a life slice

- What is the fastest affordable communication method?
- Who has access to it?
- What does a long-distance call cost relative to wages?
- Is contact scheduled or spontaneous?
- How often does a migrant contact home?
- Can the household reach the migrant in an emergency?
- Does job information arrive by letter, phone, newspaper, recruiter or personal network?
- Can money be sent without physically returning?
- Does communication technology change how often the person travels home?

## Literature question

When a character leaves home, modern readers often unconsciously give them a smartphone.

Remove it.

Ask:

- How does the family know where they are?
- How can plans change?
- How long does uncertainty last?
- What does a missed letter or telegram mean?
- Could someone simply phone before traveling?

The emotional structure of migration can change dramatically.

## Comparative research agenda

- village landline adoption
- public telephone shops
- long-distance calling prices
- pager adoption
- mobile phone ownership by rural/urban households
- SMS and migrant communication
- internet cafes and migrant job search
- remittance technologies
- smartphones and video calling
- how communication changes frequency of home visits

The central principle is:

> **Transport lowers the cost of moving bodies. Communication lowers the cost of keeping relationships alive across distance.**
