# AI as cognitive infrastructure

Generative AI should not be studied only as a software product category.

For ordinary-life history, the more important question is:

> **When does AI become infrastructure for thinking in the same way that search engines became infrastructure for finding information?**

## From tool to infrastructure

A tool is something a person deliberately opens for a particular task.

Infrastructure is something whose presence quietly changes the expected way a task is done.

A calculator becomes infrastructure when arithmetic is designed around its availability.

A search engine becomes infrastructure when homework, journalism and office work assume that facts can be retrieved quickly.

Generative AI approaches infrastructure status when people begin to assume that they can always obtain:

- a first explanation;
- an outline;
- a rewrite;
- a translation;
- a code example;
- debugging help;
- brainstorming;
- summarization;
- a second opinion;
- a draft.

The important historical transition is not simply mass user count.

It is when tasks are redesigned around the expectation that AI assistance exists.

## The adoption curve is extremely fast

OpenAI publicly introduced ChatGPT on 30 November 2022.

Source:

- https://openai.com/index/chatgpt/

CNNIC reported that by December 2024, **249 million people in China had used generative-AI products**, equivalent to 17.7% of the population.

By June 2025, the reported user population reached **515 million** with 36.5% penetration.

By December 2025, it reached **602 million**, or 42.8% penetration.

Sources:

- CNNIC 55th Internet Statistical Report: https://www3.cnnic.cn/n4/2025/0117/c88-11229.html
- CNNIC Generative AI Application Development Report 2025: https://www3.cnnic.cn/n4/2025/1021/c88-11391.html
- CNNIC 57th Internet Statistical Report: https://www3.cnnic.cn/n4/2026/0304/c88-11549.html

That is an unusually compressed infrastructure transition.

## Youth encounter it first

CNNIC's 2024 report showed generative-AI use among Chinese Internet users at:

- ages 19 and under: 29.1%
- ages 20–29: 40.5%
- ages 30–39: 23.7%
- ages 40–49: 17.1%
- ages 50–59: 10.4%
- ages 60+: 5.4%

Source:

- https://www.cnnic.cn/NMediaFile/2025/1210/MAIN176533268100200W084JJXA.pdf

The age profile matters because young users are still forming habits of study and work.

AI does not merely accelerate established routines. It can shape the routines before they become stable.

## Cognitive infrastructure changes the first move

Before search engines, the first move might be:

> ask a teacher, open an encyclopedia, visit a library.

After search engines:

> type keywords.

With generative AI:

> describe the problem in natural language.

That difference sounds small but changes several cognitive tasks.

The system can:

- infer what the user is asking;
- propose structure;
- synthesize multiple concepts;
- transform tone;
- continue dialogue;
- adapt explanations to follow-up questions.

The user's work moves partly from retrieval toward **problem formulation and evaluation**.

## The danger of invisible substitution

Infrastructure becomes historically powerful when it is difficult to notice what it replaced.

If a student always begins an essay by asking AI for an outline, they may not remember what it feels like to organize a large blank problem alone.

If a programmer always pastes an error into an AI assistant, they may not remember debugging by reading documentation, searching forums and manually isolating a fault.

If translation always begins with a generated version, the experience of wrestling sentence by sentence with ambiguity changes.

This does not prove skill loss.

It means the skill-development pathway has changed and must be studied directly.

## AI can be several infrastructures at once

### Tutor

Explains concepts and answers follow-up questions.

### Clerk

Formats, summarizes, rewrites and organizes information.

### Drafting partner

Produces first-pass text or code.

### Search intermediary

Provides synthesized answers before the user sees original sources.

### Simulator

Role-plays interviewer, examiner, customer or collaborator.

### Automation layer

Connects with tools and workflows to execute repeated tasks.

The historical meaning depends on which role becomes ordinary for which population.

## Verification becomes a central literacy

Traditional search exposes the user to multiple visible documents.

Generative AI can collapse several sources or inferred patterns into one fluent response.

The response may look finished even when uncertain.

Therefore AI literacy requires:

- asking for sources;
- opening primary material;
- checking numbers;
- distinguishing direct evidence from synthesis;
- noticing uncertainty;
- testing code;
- comparing outputs;
- knowing enough domain knowledge to challenge plausible nonsense.

A paradox appears:

> **The easier it becomes to obtain an answer, the more valuable the ability to know whether an answer deserves trust.**

## Cognitive offloading is not new

Writing offloads memory.

Books offload recall.

Calculators offload arithmetic.

Search engines offload retrieval.

Navigation apps offload route memory.

AI extends offloading into more complex cognitive operations:

- phrasing;
- planning;
- synthesis;
- coding;
- explanation;
- comparison.

The correct historical question is not whether offloading is good or bad.

It is:

> **Which mental operations cease to be routinely practiced, which new operations become important, and who benefits most from the trade?**

## AI also changes time expectations

Once a first draft can appear in seconds, the expected turnaround time of work may shrink.

That creates a familiar infrastructure effect.

Email made messages faster but also created faster response expectations.

Smartphones made workers reachable but increased reachability expectations.

AI may make drafting faster while raising the expected volume and polish of output.

So productivity gains can become a new baseline rather than leisure.

## AI changes the apprenticeship problem

Junior workers historically learned by doing routine tasks repeatedly.

If AI performs much of the routine first pass, organizations may gain efficiency but lose part of the learning ladder.

Questions to track:

- What tasks did junior staff perform before AI?
- Which were tedious but educational?
- Which are now delegated?
- How do juniors acquire domain intuition if repetitive first-pass work disappears?
- Does AI tutoring replace some of that learning?
- Who checks the AI if both junior and senior assume the system is right?

## AI can widen and narrow inequality simultaneously

It can lower barriers by providing cheap tutoring, translation and coding help.

But strong users often benefit more because they can:

- formulate better questions;
- detect errors;
- connect outputs to domain knowledge;
- automate workflows;
- afford better tools;
- work in institutions that permit advanced use.

Thus AI may reduce the cost of basic assistance while increasing the returns to judgment and orchestration.

## Education begins to institutionalize AI literacy

China's Ministry of Education issued guidance in 2024 for integrating AI literacy across primary and secondary education.

In 2026, an interdepartmental AI + Education Action Plan called for AI courses to be expanded in schools and for AI to become a public foundational course in universities.

Sources:

- https://en.moe.gov.cn/news/press_releases/202412/t20241210_1166454.html
- https://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A16/s3342/202604/t20260410_1433240.html

When schools formally teach how to use AI, the technology has moved beyond novelty.

It is becoming part of the expected competence of citizenship and work.

## What a future historian should preserve

Do not only archive model names and benchmark scores.

Preserve:

- screenshots of first AI-assisted homework;
- prompts students actually wrote;
- teacher rules on acceptable use;
- assignments redesigned because AI exists;
- code before and after AI assistance;
- interview preparation conversations;
- job descriptions mentioning AI literacy;
- workplace policies on AI use;
- moments when users discovered AI hallucination;
- personal accounts of feeling more capable, more dependent, or both.

The historical object is not merely the model.

It is the **new division of cognitive labor between person and machine**.
