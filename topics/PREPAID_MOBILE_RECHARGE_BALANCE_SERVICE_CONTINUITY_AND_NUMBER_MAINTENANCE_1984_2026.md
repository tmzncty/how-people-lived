# From "buying a telephone card" to "spending a few yuan a month to keep this number": prepaid, top-up and communication continuity (China, 1984–2026)

> Cross-repo companion: `tmzncty/old-web-archaeology/docs/MOBILE_PREPAID_RECHARGE_CARD_ONLINE_TOPUP_BALANCE_AND_SERVICE_CONTINUITY_STATE_GAPS_1999_2015.md`
>
> Related existing topic: `PHONE_NUMBER_REACHABILITY_PORTABILITY_ACCOUNT_BINDING_AND_NUMBER_RECYCLING_1987_2026.md`

## 0. Why this is not a "history of mobile tariffs"

An existing phone-number topic has already answered another set of questions: how a number turned from a place-bound contact point into a social address that follows a person, and how it went on sedimenting into an entry point for banks, schools, hospitals, Internet accounts, verification codes and password recovery.

This topic does not repeat that line. It asks about the plainer condition underneath it:

> **The fact that a person already has a mobile number does not mean that this contact point will keep working. How does an ordinary person keep putting money into a communication account, manage the balance, find top-up channels across places, and hold their own reachability online amid arrears, suspension, risk controls and long-term number retention?**

This belongs to life horizons rather than to operator product history because ordinary people's long journeys, job searches, care work, family remittances, school notices, client contact and, later, a great deal of digital identity all came to rest on "this number still being alive tomorrow".

This topic therefore connects money to communication continuity, instead of counting which plan was cheap.

---

## 1. Core concepts

### 1.1 `prepaid reachability`

The right to communicate is not settled after the fact. A consumable balance has to sit in the account first, and the state of that balance directly participates in deciding whether the next call, text message or data session can execute.

The minimum state chain:

```text
number allocated / SIM usable
→ account has usable credit?
→ network authorizes service?
→ charge deducted in/near real time
→ remaining credit above service threshold?
→ next communication remains executable?
```

Therefore:

```text
having a phone ≠ having usable communication service
having a number ≠ being able to originate calls or use data right now
an account that still has records ≠ service that has definitely not entered a restricted or suspended state
```

### 1.2 `top-up labor`

The series of practical labors a user has to complete in order to keep the account usable:

```text
noticing the balance is insufficient
→ find a business hall/newsstand/small shop/recharge card/telephone banking/Web/App
→ prepare cash, a bank card or a payment account
→ confirm the number and where it belongs
→ enter the card number and PIN, or pay
→ wait for backend crediting
→ confirm the balance/service is restored
```

The fewer the channels, the narrower the opening hours, and the more urgent the situation when the person is away from home or it is late at night, the heavier the top-up labor.

### 1.3 `remote recharge delegation`

Money can be paid into a mobile account by a person in another place.

This is not a marginal feature. For migrant workers, students studying away from home, household members and business travelers, it means:

> **When communication service runs short, the number holder does not necessarily have to go back to the local area and buy a physical card; family, friends or a bank account can restore this reachability chain on their behalf.**

### 1.4 `service-continuity buffer`

The room that an operator, an institution or an account design allows for "the money was not topped up in time, but communication does not immediately break", for example:

- credit opening (service started on credit);
- deferred suspension;
- no suspension for arrears during disasters or epidemics;
- low-balance reminders and grace.

It has a direct analogy with `runway` in the cash-buffer topic:

```text
cash buffer buys time before life must change
communication buffer buys time before reachability disappears
```

### 1.5 `identity-maintenance floor`

By the late 2010s, the main value of some old numbers was no longer "I want to make a lot of calls on it", but:

```text
old friends still know this number
+ banks/government/schools/hospitals have had it on file
+ a large number of Internet accounts are bound to it
+ verification codes and recovery chains still depend on it
= even at very low communication usage, it is worth paying the minimum monthly cost to keep holding it
```

This is not a legal judgment that a phone number itself "becomes an asset". It is an exit cost in life history.

### 1.6 `funded-account fallacy`

It cannot be written as:

```text
topped up / balance positive → service definitely usable
```

The actual service state may also depend on:

- real-name registration and identity verification for the number;
- anti-fraud and abnormal-use risk controls;
- SIM or handset state;
- plan and account lifecycle;
- operator faults;
- regional and roaming conditions;
- whether the number has already entered a protective suspension or deactivation process.

So "money" is only one gate on communication executability, not the only gate.

## 2. Before the Internet: buying "communication time" in advance predates mobile top-up

### 2.1 1980s–1990s: public telephone cards show that "prepaid communication" is not a mobile-Internet invention

A 2018 Yangcheng Evening News retrospective on the history of telephone cards states that Shenzhen introduced magnetic-card payphones and issued telephone magnetic cards in 1984; 2006 Xinhua material also records that nationally usable telephone magnetic cards began to be issued in 1994 and were later replaced by IC telephone cards.

Sources:
- Yangcheng Evening News / Sina Guangdong, 2018-08-20: https://gd.sina.com.cn/news/2018-08-20/detail-ihhxaafy8972906.shtml
- Xinhua Net / Sina, 2006-09-22: https://news.sina.com.cn/c/cul/2006-09-22/111310084370s.shtml

**Evidence:** C/B retrospective chronology. Suitable for the narrow claim that stored-value/paid-in-advance public telephony existed before mass mobile recharge; not suitable for prevalence estimates.

The most important distinction here is not whether a card existed, but where the credit and the identity were attached:

```text
public telephone card:
value attaches mainly to a portable card
+ can be used at many public terminals
+ usually does not represent a long-term reachable address belonging to an individual

prepaid mobile phone:
value attaches to an operator account/number
+ the balance decides whether the personal number can keep communicating
+ the number itself gradually becomes a social address
```

In other words, the telephone card had already made a "communication budget" portable; prepaid mobile communication then joined that budget to **an address at which a particular person could be found over the long term**.

### 2.2 Do not treat 1994 as the starting point of all digital communication

This topic follows the repository's existing boundaries:

- 20 April 1994 is an important node in China's full-function connection to the international Internet;
- before that date there were already telephones, pagers, mobile communication, research e-mail and other digital communication;
- after 1994 it also does not follow that ordinary households could immediately top up a mobile phone through the Web.

The prehistory in this topic therefore belongs mainly to telecommunications and payment infrastructure; what actually enters `old-web-archaeology` begins only once top-up starts passing through online service halls, third-party payment, C2C platforms and web transaction interfaces.

## 3. An overseas comparison: prepaid turns "credit eligibility" into "balance eligibility"

### 3.1 1990s: this was not a China-specific phenomenon

The ITU's 1999 *World Telecommunication Development Report* treated prepaid as an important mechanism of mobile expansion at the time, and noted that it mattered especially to people whose income, location or credit conditions made traditional fixed or postpaid service hard to obtain; prepaid reduced operator billing, collection and credit risk, and also made spending easier for users to control.

Sources:
- ITU, *World Telecommunication Development Report 1999*: https://www.itu.int/ITU-D/ict/publications/wtdr_99/material/wtdr99s.pdf
- ITU, *Trends in Telecommunication Reform 1999*: https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-d/opb/pref/D-PREF-TTR.2-1999-PDF-E.pdf

**Evidence:** A/B institutional contemporaneous synthesis.

Vodafone's 1999 Form 20-F filed with the SEC records that it pioneered a contract-free mobile telephone service in the UK in 1996, relaunched it as `Pay As You Talk` in 1997, and that by 1999 prepaid had become an important part of rapid subscriber growth.

Source:
- Vodafone 1999 Form 20-F: https://www.vodafone.com/content/dam/vodcom/files/investors/annual-report/20f/pt2/1999_20-f.pdf

**Evidence:** A company regulatory filing. Proves company product chronology and claimed customer figures, not universal user experience.

The shared global change was therefore not simply that "phones became cheaper", but:

```text
postpaid eligibility:
identity/address/contract/billing/credit relationship

→ prepaid eligibility:
pay first + keep the balance/account valid
```

The entry threshold fell, but part of the routine maintenance responsibility also shifted from the operator's end-of-month billing system to the user: **you had to remember yourself whether there was still money in the account.**

## 4. 1999–2004: for the first time the balance itself becomes the gate to personal reachability

### 4.1 1999: "Ruyitong" states the status relationship very clearly

On 15 December 1999, China Unicom announced the launch of its "Ruyitong" prepaid service in Beijing, Shanghai and Guangzhou. A contemporaneous China News Service report described the mechanism clearly:

- at call setup, acceptance or rejection is decided according to the account balance;
- calls are charged and deducted in real time;
- users can top up the prepaid account at any time;
- no basic monthly fee and no network access fee are charged;
- there is no need to pay at a fixed time and a fixed place;
- users can control call spending in advance.

Source:
- China News Service / Sina, 1999-12-16: https://news.sina.com.cn/china/1999-12-16/42641.html

**Evidence:** B contemporaneous wire report relaying an operator announcement; strong for product mechanics, weaker for actual household uptake.

This step genuinely wrote a person's reachability as an account state:

```text
NUMBER_EXISTS
+ NETWORK_COVERAGE
+ ACCOUNT_BALANCE
→ CALL AUTHORIZATION
```

It produced two opposite effects on ordinary life:

1. no postpaid bill, fixed payment date or traditional credit arrangement was needed, which made mobile communication easier to start using;
2. once the balance was exhausted, communication could suddenly lose the ability to originate calls at the very moment it was most needed.

So "more flexible" also meant "more responsibility for monitoring the balance".

### 4.2 A physical recharge card was a real logistics system

Contemporaneous material from 2004–2005 still shows recharge cards existing as everyday physical goods: local distribution points, small shops and street stalls held cards of different denominations; a card itself also carried a serial number, visible code and hidden code, plus a verification relationship with the carrier's backend.

Sources:
- Legal Evening News / Sina, 2005-03-10 (Beijing Mobile lowers the minimum recharge-card denomination): https://tech.sina.com.cn/t/2005-03-10/1838547300.shtml
- Hanwang / Sina, 2005-10-29 (case of a recharge card swapped at a small shop): https://news.sina.com.cn/s/2005-10-29/10487301751s.shtml
- Shanghai Youth Daily / Sina, 2006-06-22 (case material on a recharge system, providing clues about card data fields): https://tech.sina.com.cn/t/2006-06-22/10511002972.shtml

**Evidence:** B contemporaneous media.

There is a real chain here that today's "one tap to top up" easily hides:

```text
operator creates recharge credential
→ distributor receives stock
→ small retailer obtains card
→ user must reach retailer during usable time
→ pays cash
→ reveals/scratches credential
→ submits PIN through carrier channel
→ carrier backend accepts/rejects
→ balance changes
```

A recharge card was therefore both a financial credential and an object that had to circulate through a real city.

## 5. 2005–2012: the expansive future — "I don't have to be there to keep this communication chain going"

The freedom added at this stage was not simply "faster top-up" but the unbinding of top-up from a particular physical place.

### 5.1 2006: migrants were already using "a friend tops it up" to keep an old number

A 2006 Southern Weekly report on Mr Xu's dispute left behind a very valuable first-person, contemporaneous life path:

- he was transferred from Suzhou to Shanghai in 2005;
- to keep his business relationships, his Suzhou Unicom mobile number stayed active;
- because he was in Shanghai, he often asked a friend to top up that phone.

Source:
- Southern Weekly / Sina, 2006-06-15: https://tech.sina.com.cn/t/2006-06-15/1137991772.shtml

**Evidence:** B contemporaneous media with first-person participant testimony.

This material shows:

> **"An old number following the person" did not depend only on nationwide roaming; it also depended on a payment and social collaboration arrangement that could keep putting money into it from another place.**

If top-up had still required the person to return to the home region, the spatial portability of the number would have been tied back to a place by the need to maintain its funding.

### 5.2 2006: the bank card turns "find a card" into "remotely credit the account"

In 2006 Beijing Unicom, ICBC and Yeepay launched bank-card telephone top-up. A user first linked a bank account to a mobile number through ICBC telephone banking, then dialed the top-up line to complete the recharge; one ICBC account could top up several mobile numbers.

Sources:
- Communications World / Sina, 2006-06-14: https://tech.sina.com.cn/t/2006-06-14/1615990255.shtml
- Beijing Times / Sina, 2006-06-16: https://tech.sina.com.cn/t/2006-06-16/0546992912.shtml
- ChinaTechNews, 2006-06-19: https://www.chinatechnews.com/2006/06/19/3901-beijing-unicom-cooperates-with-yeepay-and-icbc-on-e-payment

**Evidence:** B contemporaneous industry/media; several reports share the same launch event, so they are not counted as fully independent evidence families.

What actually happened here was:

```text
physical recharge-card credential
→ bank account + telephone identity/number binding
→ remote payment instruction
→ credit to the carrier account
```

And "one bank account can top up several numbers" meant that family care could pass through this interface too: parents topping up a child living elsewhere, adults topping up for the elderly, friends providing emergency help — none of these any longer required buying a card and relaying a string of scratched-off digits.

### 5.3 2005–2007: the Web also enters the top-up chain

In 2005 Zhejiang Unicom connected third-party online payment into the payment page of its online service hall. The contemporaneous report gives the historical host as `www.zj.chinaunicom.com`.

Source:
- eNet / Sina, 2005-12-13: https://tech.sina.com.cn/roll/2005-12-13/1013790358.shtml

**Evidence:** B contemporaneous service report. The historical page itself has not been verified in this slice.

In 2007 Taobao's release of its 2006 transaction data listed "mobile top-up / IP cards" among the platform's best-selling categories; related reports said that the recharge-card transaction value of Taobao alone was already considerable.

Sources:
- Qianlong / Sohu, 2007-02-06: https://news.sohu.com/20070206/n248072994.shtml
- Economic Observer, 2007-04-09: https://www.eeo.com.cn/industry/it_telecomm/2007/04/09/54010.html

**Evidence:** B; platform figures originate from Taobao and should be treated as company-reported platform scale, not population penetration.

This step suits old-Web archaeology well: a "communication credential" that once had to be bought at a newsstand became a digital good that could be searched, paid for and delivered immediately inside a C2C platform or an online service hall.

### 5.4 New convenience produces new fraud: `payment success != carrier credit`

In December 2007, after seeing "discounted mobile recharge cards", Ms Zhang was lured to a fake payment page and entered her bank-card details; her card was fraudulently charged within seconds.

Source:
- The Beijing News / Sina, 2008-01-11: https://news.sina.com.cn/c/2008-01-11/014313236252s.shtml

**Evidence:** B contemporaneous police/media case.

In 2010, because the business hall had already closed and she urgently needed to top up, Ms Bai spent 60 yuan at a roadside stall. She received a voice prompt saying "recharge successful, current balance 100 yuan"; the next day her phone was suspended, and only then did she find that the money had never actually been credited to the carrier account.

Source:
- Hebei Youth Daily / Sina, 2010-08-16: https://news.sina.com.cn/c/2010-08-16/070117970386s.shtml

**Evidence:** B contemporaneous first-person complaint.

This material is extremely useful for fixing a hard boundary:

```text
the user hears/sees "recharge successful"
≠ the carrier's authoritative ledger has already been credited
≠ service will still be usable the next day
```

Digitalization reduced card-hunting and queuing, but it also exposed users to a provenance problem between a "fabricated success screen or voice prompt" and the real carrier backend.

### 5.5 2010: top-up begins to be embedded in mobile banking itself

In 2010 China Construction Bank's mobile banking introduced nationwide mobile top-up, which could top up one's own number or another number and returned a voucher number and payment date.

Source:
- China Construction Bank, 2010-07-28: https://www2.ccb.com/chn/2010-07/28/article_2021122410374130178.shtml

**Evidence:** A contemporaneous bank service documentation.

It compressed the resolution path for "communication is about to be cut off" even further:

```text
find a business hall/small shop
→ find telephone banking
→ find a web page
→ directly top up this phone or another phone from the mobile banking app already in hand
```

This is what `recharge-location decoupling` really means.

## 6. 2013–2014: a strict balance threshold begins to acquire a "credit opening" buffer

Before the 2013 Spring Festival, China Mobile described a set of deferred-suspension and credit-opening measures. The material notes specifically that for users of Shenzhouxing, M-Zone and similar plans, when the account balance fell to a certain low level and there was no time to top up, they could apply for a certain credit value according to years on the network; ordinary GoTone users would also, on particular holidays, have the two-way suspension caused by arrears postponed.

Source:
- Zhejiang Mobile reprint of group convenience services, 2013-02-09: https://www.zj.10086.cn/aboutus/xwdt/200000876227.html

**Evidence:** A/B operator contemporaneous documentation; regional rules must not be extrapolated into a permanent nationwide uniform rule.

This is a very important state change:

```text
traditional prepaid intuition:
balance hits threshold → service immediately contracts/stops

once a continuity buffer appears:
balance insufficient
→ user credit/holiday policy/grace conditions
→ temporarily still reachable
→ afterwards top up or return to normal settlement
```

An ordinary person's communication reachability began to possess a "short-term buffer", instead of jumping instantly and entirely with the account balance.

## 7. 2015–2019: the congested future — from "how many minutes do I have left" to "I simply cannot easily lose this number"

### 7.1 The prepaid/top-up problem changes semantically

In the 2000s, topping up mainly solved:

> "I want to keep making calls and sending texts, so there has to be money in the account."

In the late 2010s, with dual-SIM phones, mobile-data competition and Internet account binding, some low-usage numbers acquired a different maintenance logic:

> "I rarely use it for data or outgoing calls, but too many people and too many systems remember it, so I want to keep it existing at the lowest possible cost."

### 7.2 2018: the "number-retention plan" publicly names this life strategy

In 2018 China Mobile's 8-yuan low-threshold plan was briefly taken off the shelf and then restored. A contemporaneous Beijing Youth Daily / People's Daily Online report records that some users took the low-price plan precisely in order to keep receiving calls, text messages, verification codes and messages from old contacts who did not know they had changed numbers, while moving their main communication to another card.

Sources:
- Beijing Youth Daily / People's Daily Online, 2018-09-25: https://finance.people.com.cn/n1/2018/0925/c1004-30310739.html
- Xinhua Net reprint, 2018-09-25: https://www.xinhuanet.com/politics/2018-09/25/c_1123476111.htm

**Evidence:** B contemporaneous media; the quoted explanation of usage partly comes from industry observations, not a representative survey.

This stage allows us to propose:

`identity-maintenance floor`

```text
monthly payment
not primarily buying more conversation
but buying continued possession of an address
```

This is mutually causal with the existing `account-binding debt` topic:

- the more bindings there are, the harder the number is to cancel;
- the harder the number is to cancel, the more willing the user is to pay a low monthly fee to keep it;
- the longer it is kept, the more bindings it may go on generating.

### 7.3 A counter-example: "number retention" must not be romanticized as freedom

A cheap retention plan means the user simultaneously takes on:

- additional SIM and plan management;
- a fixed monthly cost;
- plan changes and customer-service labor;
- multi-number notification and verification-code routing;
- if they forget to keep paying, the arrears–suspension–deactivation chain still awaits.

So "dual SIM + retention" looks more like a workaround than proof that digital identity has genuinely become portable.

## 8. 2020–2022: the episodic future — in a crisis, communication is temporarily decoupled from payment status

During the 2020 epidemic, the three major carriers publicly introduced measures such as no suspension, deferred suspension, postponed suspension and emergency reconnection. A China Economic Net / People's Daily Online report summarized this as "no suspension for arrears" during epidemic prevention and control, and provided coverage counts in the carriers' own terms.

Sources:
- China Economic Net / People's Daily Online, 2020-02-05: https://energy.people.com.cn/GB/n1/2020/0205/c71661-31572218.html
- Hubei Daily, 2020-02-04: https://m.cnhubei.com/content/2020-02/04/content_12687344.html

**Evidence:** A/B contemporaneous institutional/media reports. Large "person-times/households" figures are company or official operational counts and should not be treated as deduplicated population estimates.

What matters most here is not the numbers, but that the normal state machine was deliberately rewritten:

```text
NORMAL:
arrears reach the condition → restriction/suspension

EMERGENCY:
arrears reach the condition
→ policy override / grace
→ service remains available or is urgently restored
```

This shows that by 2020 communication continuity was no longer merely a private consumption convenience; under conditions where staying at home, remote work, medical coordination, health codes and notifications, and public information all depended heavily on mobile phones, **suddenly losing communication would itself amplify the crisis.**

So "no suspension for arrears" can be understood as a temporary protection of social infrastructure.

But this does not prove that after the epidemic all users permanently obtained the same grace.

## 9. 2023–2026: the defensive future — keeping a number alive is no longer just about topping up

### 9.1 `funded account != reachable account`

In 2024 a Shenzhen Mobile user said on social media that a number he had used for 14 years had been subject to a "protective suspension", and that restoring it at the business hall required supplementary identity documents, proof of residence or social-insurance participation and similar materials. Follow-up media reporting obtained the local business hall's explanation of anti-fraud risk-control requirements.

Sources:
- Huashang Daily reprint / Sina Finance, 2024-07-22: https://finance.sina.com.cn/jjxw/2024-07-22/doc-incezcxv8804501.shtml
- Other reprints of this incident are not used as an independent evidence family.

**Evidence:** B contemporaneous first-person account plus media follow-up. Specific local handling requirements should not be generalized to all regions or operators.

This material supplies the new state machine of the 2020s:

```text
the user is willing to keep paying
+ the number has been in long-term use
+ the account may not even be in arrears
≠ the risk-control system will necessarily allow communication to continue
```

Communication continuity now depends simultaneously on at least:

```text
funding state
+ identity/KYC state
+ risk-control state
+ device/SIM state
+ network state
```

### 9.2 Exit also becomes a problem that requires governance

In 2025 the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology promoted a "second-hand number renewal" service that allows the user of a newly assigned number to initiate unbinding of historical Internet accounts; the service has already covered a large number of commonly used applications.

Source:
- CCTV.com, 2025-05-02: https://news.cctv.com/2025/05/02/ARTIuXRzE3Q0owgwGndKozji250502.shtml

**Evidence:** A/B current institutional/media documentation.

The detailed mechanism here is already covered by the `PHONE_NUMBER_REACHABILITY...` topic; this topic uses it only to confirm one consequence:

> **By the 2020s, continuing to pay the minimum fee every month for an old number is sometimes not because the number itself still has much communication demand, but because "safely exiting this number" still requires cleaning up a large amount of cross-platform state.**

This is precisely a typical move of the defensive future:

```text
not cutting off the old infrastructure as fast as possible
but first maintaining the old entry point at low cost
while slowly migrating/unbinding/building fallbacks
```

## 10. Comparison of four kinds of "future"

| Period | The main meaning of top-up/balance in ordinary life | Risks generated at the same time |
|---|---|---|
| 2005–2012 expansive | "I can keep a distant number going right away without going to a business hall; online payment will keep getting more convenient" | fake top-ups, payment fraud, success states that disagree across systems |
| 2015–2019 congested | "The number has too many bindings, so even if I barely use it I keep it cheaply for now" | multiple cards, multiple plans, binding debt and continuous management labor |
| 2020–2022 episodic | "Even if the normal payment chain breaks for a while, communication must not vanish immediately" | temporary policy easily confused with a permanent right |
| 2023–2026 defensive | "Keep the old identity entry point while preparing migration, unbinding and risk-control fallbacks" | money alone may still not preserve service because of identity/risk control; exit costs are high |

This change can be condensed into:

```text
money buys minutes
→ money buys continuous reachability
→ money buys continued possession of identity-bearing number
→ payment alone is no longer sufficient; continuity needs fallback and state hygiene
```

## 11. Life-matrix mapping

### Education

When students live away from home, family remote top-up lowers the cost of recovering from "communication balance exhausted → losing contact with home"; but this cannot be extrapolated to prevalence from a single case.

### Employment and informal labor

Salespeople, self-employed operators, temporary workers and job seekers depend especially on a number that stays reachable. One day of suspension may be more than an inconvenience: it can mean a missed client or a missed interview.

### Migration

Mr Xu's 2006 Suzhou → Shanghai case shows that spatial portability of a number must be matched by portability of its funding maintenance.

### Care and the household

"Topping up for someone else" turns a small act of family care into a remotely executable action: communication itself needs money, and money can also rescue communication across networks.

### Long-term interruption

Deferred suspension in a crisis is a buffer against "short-term economic or payment interruption → long-term social disconnection".

### Documents and records

In the 2020s, communication continuity is increasingly affected by real-name registration, risk control and number lifecycle; the balance is only one layer of that state.

### Modes of communication

The core turning point:

```text
portable credit on a public telephone card
→ balance in a personal mobile account
→ remote/online top-up
→ credit/grace buffer
→ the minimum maintenance fee for a number as identity infrastructure
```

### Internet platforms and online identity

The more a mobile number becomes the entry point for verification codes and recovery, the more the cost of retaining it resembles identity rent; but the specific binding mechanism is handled by the companion topic.

### Information access and social relationships

Arrears or suspension of a number cut off not only voice, but also notifications, text messages, verification codes, and the ability to reconnect social relationships.

### Expectations about the future

2000s: the phone credit can be topped up anytime, anywhere;
late 2010s: the number had better not be lost just because the main SIM is replaced;
2020s: key entry points need low-cost maintenance, grace and an exit plan.

### Platform disappearance and memory preservation

Recharge vouchers, transaction pages, balance interfaces and carrier backends are different objects; an old web page can preserve a top-up entry point, but usually cannot preserve an authoritative balance mutation.

## 12. Counter-examples and conclusions that cannot be drawn

### Counter-example A: prepaid lowers the threshold, which does not mean it is cheaper

China Unicom's own 1999 material pointed out that the absence of a monthly fee or network access fee does not mean the unit communication tariff is necessarily lower. The value of prepayment includes the credit threshold, spending control and flexibility; it cannot automatically be equated with the lowest total cost.

### Counter-example B: more convenient top-up does not mean communication never breaks

Backend faults, fraud, risk controls, and SIM or identity problems can still make service unusable.

### Counter-example C: more electronic channels does not mean every person suits electronic channels

Physical business halls, recharge points, telephone banking, SMS, the Web, WAP and mobile banking coexisted for a long time, which is precisely evidence that different people have different devices, bank cards, network conditions, digital skills and trust conditions.

### Counter-example D: retaining a number does not mean the old number is permanently safe

If the user eventually stops paying, fails to deal with real-name or risk-control requirements, or actively cancels, the number still enters the lifecycle of suspension—deactivation—freezing—reassignment.

### Counter-example E: no suspension for arrears during a crisis is not a permanent institution

It proves that communication continuity can be given priority protection in a special period; it does not prove that the ordinary rules have been permanently abolished.

## 13. Hindsight risks

### Risk 1: writing the 2000 mobile number as today's OTP identity infrastructure

What ordinary users cared about most directly in 2000 was calls, text messages and balance; the account-binding debt of the 2020s cannot be projected back onto all early users.

### Risk 2: writing the 2007 scale of Taobao top-ups as everyone topping up online

Platform transaction data only proves that a new channel existed conspicuously; a large number of users still relied on business halls, newsstands, small shops, telephone banking and recharge cards.

### Risk 3: treating the 2018 "number-retention plan" as a strategy for everyone

It proves that this behavior was enough to enter public discussion; it does not provide a national share of users.

### Risk 4: writing the history of top-up as a linear history of App progress

Physical cards, telephone banking, web pages, WAP, SMS, third-party payment, mobile banking and business halls did not die off as soon as the next one appeared; they overlapped for a long time.

## 14. Still unresolved

1. For the 1980s–1990s, there is still no nationally comparable dataset on the ordinary use distribution of magnetic-card and IC public telephones across different cities.
2. For 1999–2002, the precise regional rules of Shenzhouxing/Ruyitong in the early period ("insufficient balance—one-way suspension—two-way suspension—deactivation") still require original user manuals or tariff documents.
3. For the 2005 Zhejiang Unicom `www.zj.chinaunicom.com` online top-up page, this round did not obtain a historical archive capture that can be verified item by item.
4. There is no scale data on how much of early online top-up was "paying for oneself" versus "family/friends paying on one's behalf".
5. For post-2018 low-cost "number retention" behavior, reliable statistics on the real scale and social distribution of users are lacking.
6. The local implementation conditions of the 2024 protective-suspension case cannot be generalized into a uniform national rule.

## 15. What this changes about our understanding of "how people lived"

The freedom that communication infrastructure gives ordinary people does not come only from "how far the network coverage reaches".

What can actually turn a number into a long-term interface of life also requires a very inconspicuous maintenance capacity: **when the balance is short today, can I keep this contact point alive without going back to the original place, without waiting for the business hall to open, and even without operating it myself?**

The expansion of the 2000s made top-up look more and more like a small action executable at any moment; it made it easier for people studying, working or doing business away from home to keep their existing contact point. By the late 2010s, the number had sedimented so many verification codes, banks, clients and old contacts that some people began paying the minimum fee every month, not to "buy more calls" but to renew the lease on an address that had already grown into their life course.

The 2020 "no suspension for arrears" then exposed the real social meaning of this infrastructure: when ordinary life is suddenly interrupted, what people most need is not necessarily more communication plans, but **that a payment not completed in time should not pull a person out of relationships, work, public information and help chains all at once.**

By the middle of the 2020s, the problem had gone even further: paying is itself no longer a guarantee of reachability. Users also have to maintain real-name registration, risk-control and cross-platform binding state, and prepare a migration plan for eventually exiting the old number.

So this history is not:

> "Top-up keeps getting more convenient."

It is:

> **"Ordinary people gradually learned to buy extra runtime for their own reachability; and then discovered that this reachability was no longer merely a communication service, but a layer of personal infrastructure that has to be continuously maintained and also needs a safe way to exit."**
