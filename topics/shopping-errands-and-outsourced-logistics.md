# Shopping, errands and outsourced logistics

Ordinary-life history often records what people bought but not **how much time it took to obtain it**.

Shopping is a logistics system.

It can require:

- walking/travel;
- queueing;
- comparing shops;
- carrying goods home;
- cash withdrawal/payment;
- arranging repair;
- returning for unavailable stock;
- waiting for delivery or service workers.

Digital commerce changes this chain without making logistics disappear.

It moves some labor from the household to warehouses, couriers, platforms and service workers.

## China 2018 time-use anchor

The 2018 National Time Use Survey reports average time spent purchasing goods/services of:

- all residents: **17 minutes/day**;
- men: 12 minutes;
- women: 22 minutes;
- urban residents: 21 minutes;
- rural residents: 11 minutes.

Participation rate was 21.1%.

Among participants, average time was about 80 minutes.

The activity definition already included Internet purchasing as well as physical shopping and arranging services.

Source:

- https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202302/t20230203_1900224.html

## China 2024: shopping is explicitly hybrid

The 2024 National Time Use Survey defines "purchasing goods or services" broadly to include:

- physical-store shopping;
- Internet shopping;
- buying food/clothing/daily goods/durable goods;
- contacting repair services;
- vehicle repair;
- banking/legal/advertising/wedding/rental services;
- beauty/personal services;
- shopping through livestreams/television.

The survey reports:

- resident average: **15 minutes/day**;
- participant average: **43 minutes/day**;
- participation rate: 34.1%.

Source:

- https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202410/t20241031_1957216.html

Do not interpret 17 → 15 minutes as a precise trend without harmonizing the survey designs.

The important evidence is that the category itself has become a hybrid of physical and digital consumption.

## The disappearing errand

An older household may need a dedicated trip for:

- groceries;
- train tickets;
- bank services;
- utility payment;
- appliance repair arrangement;
- clothing;
- books;
- restaurant food.

Later systems can move many of these transactions to:

- apps;
- online marketplaces;
- digital payment;
- home delivery;
- on-demand services.

The household spends less time **going to the service**.

But someone else now performs:

- picking;
- packing;
- driving/riding;
- dispatching;
- customer-service work;
- warehouse labor.

So convenience is partly **labor relocation**.

## Why household time can fall while consumption rises

Digital shopping lowers the fixed cost of each purchase.

If buying an item once required a one-hour trip, households might bundle purchases.

If buying requires thirty seconds in an app, households can make:

- more frequent purchases;
- smaller purchases;
- impulse purchases;
- late-night purchases;
- purchases from distant sellers.

The time cost per transaction can fall while the number of transactions rises.

This is another form of time rebound.

## Delivery changes the geography of consumption

For millennial-era youth, online marketplaces meant a town without a particular shop no longer defined the full commercial menu.

For smartphone-era youth, the important change goes further:

> the store does not merely exist elsewhere; it can arrive at the door.

This changes:

- meal planning;
- emergency purchases;
- household storage;
- acceptable distance from retail;
- working late;
- mobility needs;
- expectations of immediacy.

## Pandemic significance

The pandemic made delivery and online retail especially visible as infrastructure.

A service previously treated as convenience could become necessary for maintaining ordinary household supply when physical movement was constrained.

See:

- [`../INDEX-pandemic.md`](../INDEX-pandemic.md)

The historical lesson is that a mature delivery system can function like other infrastructure: its importance becomes most visible when normal movement is disrupted.

## Repair as a hidden logistics history

Shopping/service time should also include repair.

A household object can be useful only if:

- spare parts exist;
- repair labor is accessible;
- service can be contacted;
- transport or home service is possible.

Modern platforms can reduce the search cost of finding a repair person while also encouraging replacement when repair is inconvenient.

This should be connected to the repository's future repair-history work.

## Cohort questions

Ask a person:

- Where did your household buy groceries?
- How often did someone need to make a dedicated shopping trip?
- Who carried goods home?
- When did you first buy something online?
- When did you first order a meal rather than go collect it?
- How were bills paid?
- How was a broken appliance repaired?
- Did your town have the goods you wanted?
- When did "not sold locally" stop meaning "cannot get it"?

## Core insight

> **Convenience often means moving household logistics out of sight.**

A shorter errand does not mean less total labor exists.

It can mean the labor has moved from the consumer's visible day into a distributed network of digital systems, warehouses, transport workers and service providers.
