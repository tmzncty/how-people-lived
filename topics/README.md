# Topics

Topic notes follow everyday systems across multiple places and periods.

They are not intended to give one universal history. Their job is to provide reusable questions and comparisons for life slices.

## Current topics

- [Refrigeration](refrigeration.md) — food preservation, ice delivery, electric refrigeration, cold chains and household adoption
- [Laundry and washing machines](laundry-and-washing-machines.md) — the full labor chain behind clean clothes and the gradual mechanization of individual stages
- [Lighting and electrification](lighting-and-electrification.md) — candles, oil, gas, electric light, wiring, rural/urban electrification and the changing usable night
- [Water, sanitation and toilets](water-sanitation-and-toilets.md) — carrying water, plumbing, shared facilities, sewers, bathing and the labor hidden behind a tap
- [Cooking fuel and stoves](cooking-fuel-and-stoves.md) — wood, coal, gas, oil and electricity as household energy systems rather than just stove types
- [Post, telegraph and information delay](post-telegraph-and-information-delay.md) — collection schedules, last-mile delivery, telegrams and the lived speed of information

## How topic notes should work

A topic note should repeatedly ask:

> **What did this system require, who had access to it, and what part of an ordinary day did it change?**

For example, "electric lighting" is incomplete without electricity supply, wiring, price, bulb availability, adoption and the effect on evening activity.

Likewise, "railway opened" is incomplete without fares, frequency, station access, class, travel time and who could realistically take the train.

The best topic notes also show **coexistence**. A household can have electric light and a coal stove, piped water and an outdoor toilet, a radio and no telephone, or a washing machine that still has to be filled by hand.
