# Topics

Topic notes follow everyday systems across multiple places and periods.

They are not intended to give one universal history. Their job is to provide reusable questions and comparisons for life slices.

## How topic notes should work

A material topic note should repeatedly ask:

> **What did this system require, who had access to it, and what part of an ordinary day did it change?**

For example, "electric lighting" is incomplete without electricity supply, wiring, price, bulb availability, adoption and the effect on evening activity.

Likewise, "railway opened" is incomplete without fares, frequency, station access, class, travel time and who could realistically take the train.

A life-horizon topic should ask:

> **What did this institution or milestone make possible, and when did ordinary people begin planning around it?**

A university can exist for centuries before "I will probably go to university" becomes a normal adolescent expectation.

A job can pay less but still be preferred if it provides housing, welfare or proximity to family.

The best topic notes show **coexistence**. A household can have electric light and a coal stove, piped water and an outdoor toilet, a radio and no telephone, or a university-educated child whose parents entered work at fifteen.
