# Topics

Topic notes follow everyday systems across multiple places and periods.

They are not intended to give one universal history. Their job is to provide reusable questions and comparisons for life slices.

## How topic notes should work

A material topic note should repeatedly ask:

> **What did this system require, who had access to it, and what part of an ordinary day did it change?**

A time-use topic should ask:

> **Which activity disappeared, which became faster, who performed the remaining labor, and what new activity appeared because the old constraint was removed?**

An information-system note should ask:

> **Did information arrive on a schedule, require going somewhere, require logging in, or follow the person continuously?**

A life-horizon topic should ask:

> **What did this institution or milestone make possible, and when did ordinary people begin planning around it?**

The best topic notes show coexistence and mismatch. A household can have electric light and a coal stove, piped water and an outdoor toilet, or a university-educated child whose parents entered work at fifteen. A person can earn their own salary, live far from home and still remain financially embedded in the parental household. A worker can save time through faster communication while simultaneously becoming reachable for work during what used to be downtime.
