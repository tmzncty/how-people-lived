# What did being 20 mean in different generations?

Age is biological. The meaning of an age is historical.

A twenty-year-old in 1975, 1985, 1995, 2005, 2015 and 2025 is not standing at the same point in the life course.

This note compares the **adult problem visible at age 20** across several anchor cohorts in China.

It is not a claim that everyone had the same experience.

Urban/rural, education, family resources, gender and region remain decisive.

---

## Age 20 around 1975 — cohort born c.1955

For many people, age 20 can already be fully adult in the economic sense.

Possible realities include:

- already working;
- contributing to the household;
- preparing for or already entering marriage;
- limited realistic access to higher education;
- housing heavily constrained by family or institutional space.

The 1977 university entrance examination reopening is striking precisely because it reaches this cohort at young-adult age rather than as a routine secondary-school transition.

The education question can therefore be:

> **Can I reopen a route that was not continuously available to me?**

rather than:

> Which university should I choose?

Historical benchmark:

- 1977 exam applicants: about 5.7 million;
- admitted: 272,971;
- admission rate: 4.78%.

Source:

- https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/cndy/2015-03/31/content_19957679.htm

## Age 20 around 1985 — cohort born c.1965

Age 20 increasingly sits at a recognizable school-to-work transition, but higher education remains highly selective.

For an urban youth, a central question can be:

> **Where will I be placed or employed, and what adult life comes bundled with that position?**

A work unit can affect:

- wage;
- housing;
- welfare;
- social circle;
- pension horizon.

For a rural youth, age 20 can still be deeply embedded in household labor, farming, marriage and local work.

Household technology also remains transitional.

In 1985 urban household surveys recorded only 6.58 refrigerators and 17.21 color televisions per 100 households; rural levels were far lower.

Sources:

- urban: https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/Statisticaldata/yearlydata/YB1999e/j06e.htm
- rural: https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/Statisticaldata/yearlydata/YB2000e/J24E.htm

Thus a 20-year-old may be planning adulthood while the household itself is still being materially modernized.

## Age 20 around 1995 — cohort born c.1975

The adult problem has shifted.

Jobs and housing are beginning to unbundle.

A young adult can increasingly ask:

- Do I find a job myself?
- Can I move employers?
- Can I migrate to another city?
- Is housing something the household can own?
- If I change work, what happens to housing or benefits?

For rural youth, migration becomes a much more visible route: World Bank estimates place rural-to-urban migrant workers at 21.6 million in 1990 and 78.8 million in 2000.

Source:

- https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099020325194013546/pdf/P180848-980daabd-c4df-4314-a796-5df3bf5c97c5.pdf

The question can become:

> **Should I stay where my household is, or go somewhere else to build income?**

That is a new kind of age-20 geography.

## Age 20 around 2005 — cohort born c.1985

Higher education has expanded substantially.

The gross enrollment rate reaches 19% by 2004, compared with 3.4% in 1990.

Source:

- https://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_sjzl/moe_560/moe_1389/moe_1390/moe_1393/201002/t20100226_20347.html

For a much larger share of families, age 20 can therefore still mean:

> **student**

rather than fully established worker.

The adult problem lengthens:

- which major;
- graduate or not;
- which city;
- internship;
- first employer;
- housing later.

Economic adulthood is delayed because education itself becomes a longer investment project.

At the same time, Internet life is already part of adolescence, but smartphones have not yet fully transformed everyday reachability.

## Age 20 around 2015 — cohort born c.1995

The person enters adulthood after:

- mass higher education;
- mature Internet job search;
- smartphones and mobile social life;
- market housing as a normal adult concern;
- job mobility as an assumed possibility.

The question is no longer whether careers can change.

It is:

> **Which route has the best combination of pay, development, city, housing cost and future mobility?**

The person must manage several adult projects at once.

The first job is increasingly only one node in a longer plan.

## Age 20 around 2025 — cohort born c.2005

The person reaches age 20 after pandemic-era schooling and after generative AI becomes widely visible.

The age-20 problem now contains a cognitive layer that earlier cohorts did not face:

- How much should AI do in coursework?
- What skills remain valuable if AI can draft or code?
- How do I prove competence?
- What entry-level work still teaches me?
- Which credential remains worth pursuing?
- Do I need to learn model use as part of ordinary literacy?

The person also enters a labor market where many visible routes coexist:

- postgraduate study;
- civil-service and public-sector exams;
- private-sector work;
- manufacturing / technical work;
- flexible and platform work;
- self-employment;
- further certification.

The central question can become:

> **What should I commit to when both the labor market and the tools of thinking are changing quickly?**

# How age 20 moves later in the life course

One long-term shift is that age 20 increasingly moves from **adult settlement** toward **adult preparation** for some populations.

Earlier model:

> adolescence → work → marriage / household

Later model:

> school → university → internship / exam / further credential → first job → housing → marriage

This is not universal and should not be read as everyone becoming dependent for longer.

But educational expansion changes the institutional meaning of age 20.

## The same word “student” changes

For earlier cohorts, remaining a student at 20 can mark exceptional access.

For later cohorts, it can be normal enough that the next question becomes differentiation within education.

Thus the educational problem shifts from:

> **Can I enter?**

into:

> **What does my entry buy me relative to millions of other graduates?**

## The same word “job” changes

At age 20:

- c.1965 cohort may seek a stable institutional landing;
- c.1975 cohort may face a system beginning to allow mobility;
- c.1985 cohort expects market job search;
- c.1995 cohort expects repeated career management;
- c.2005 cohort may expect AI-assisted career management and rapidly changing skill requirements.

## The same word “independence” changes

Age 20 can mean:

- earning money;
- leaving the parental household;
- choosing one's own career;
- paying rent;
- not needing parents for housing;
- being able to support parents later.

These components do not arrive at the same age.

The history of adulthood is partly the history of these milestones becoming separated.

## Final insight

When older people say:

> “I was already doing X at twenty.”

and younger people say:

> “At twenty I was still trying to decide Y.”

the difference is not automatically maturity.

The institutions attached to age 20 may have changed.

The correct historical comparison is:

> **What did society make possible, necessary and normal at twenty for each cohort?**
