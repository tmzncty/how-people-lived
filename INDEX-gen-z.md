# Gen-Z / Post-2000 Ordinary-Life Index

This index follows young people born roughly in the early 2000s, especially the Chinese cohort born around **2000–2005**.

The purpose is not to claim that all post-2000 youth share one personality. It is to reconstruct a distinctive sequence of infrastructures and shocks:

> **internet-default childhood → smartphone adolescence → pandemic schooling / early adulthood → generative-AI adulthood**

## Why this cohort is historically distinctive

A simplified cohort born in 2003 could experience:

- age 7 in 2010: Internet and mobile phones already common background infrastructure;
- age 12 in 2015: social media, mobile video, mobile payment and app ecosystems increasingly normal;
- age 17 in 2020: school and social life disrupted by the pandemic;
- age 19 in 2022: higher education shaped by pandemic-era digitalization;
- age 20 in late 2022: conversational generative AI becomes globally visible;
- age 21–23 in 2024–2026: AI becomes ordinary in coursework, coding, research, writing and job preparation.

This produces a different historical baseline from the millennial cohort.

Millennials remember the transition from **offline → online**.

Post-2000 youth may instead remember the transition from:

> **searching for information → working with a system that generates, explains, summarizes, rewrites and reasons with them.**

## Questions this archive should eventually answer

### Childhood

- Did the child ever experience a household without broadband?
- Was a family computer shared, or was personal device ownership already common?
- When did watching television stop being tied to a fixed broadcast schedule?
- When did homework begin to assume online search?

### Adolescence

- When did the smartphone become the main social device?
- Did messaging become effectively continuous rather than session-based?
- How did short video and algorithmic recommendation change boredom and discovery?
- When did mobile payment make cash optional in everyday youth life?

### Pandemic

- Did high school or university begin or continue online?
- Which rites of passage were moved, cancelled or compressed?
- Did the family home again become the primary school environment?
- Did the cohort enter university or the labor market with pandemic routines already normalized?

### AI

- What did students first use generative AI for?
- Which tasks moved from search engines to AI dialogue?
- When did writing a first draft cease to mean starting from a blank page?
- When did debugging begin with copying an error message into an AI assistant?
- When did translation become interactive rather than sentence-by-sentence lookup?
- What new skills became important: prompting, verification, decomposition, source checking, model selection?
- Which older skills became harder to observe because AI can perform the visible first pass?

### Work

- Which entry-level tasks are automated or accelerated?
- Does AI reduce the apprenticeship value of routine junior work?
- Does it make one young worker more productive, or raise the expected baseline for everyone?
- Does a graduate now compete partly on the ability to direct AI systems?

## Core historical insight

The post-2000 cohort may be the first large cohort whose transition to adulthood includes a new question:

> **What part of thinking should I do myself, what part should I delegate, and how do I know whether the result is actually mine and actually correct?**

That question belongs in ordinary-life history just as much as the arrival of the telephone, refrigerator or personal computer.
