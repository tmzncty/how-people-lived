# 2019–2023: when ordinary distance changed

The pandemic altered not only whether people traveled, but the **mental meaning of distance**.

A trip that had once been treated as routine could become something that required contingency planning.

## A numerical baseline

Railway passenger trips provide a useful, consistent national benchmark:

| Year | Railway passenger trips | Change from previous year |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2019 | 3.66 billion | +8.4% |
| 2020 | 2.20 billion | -39.8% |
| 2021 | 2.61 billion | +18.5% |
| 2022 | 1.67 billion | -35.9% |
| 2023 | 3.85 billion | +130.4% |

Sources:

- 2019: https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202002/t20200228_1728917.html
- 2020: https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202102/t20210228_1814177.html
- 2021: https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202202/t20220227_1827963.html
- 2022: https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202302/t20230227_1918979.html
- 2023: https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202402/t20240228_1947918.html

The national total passenger-traffic series tells the same broad story:

- 2020 passenger traffic fell 45.1% from 2019;
- 2022 fell another 32.7% from 2021;
- 2023 rose 66.5% from 2022.

These are transport measures, not a complete record of household experience. But they make visible how unusual the period was.

## Before 2020: distance as schedule

A normal intercity trip could usually be planned around:

- ticket availability;
- travel time;
- money;
- annual leave;
- family schedule.

The question was mostly:

> Can I afford the trip and fit it into my calendar?

## Pandemic years: distance becomes conditional

A new question entered ordinary planning:

> **Will this trip still be feasible when the date arrives?**

This can affect:

- visiting parents;
- couples living in different cities;
- interviews;
- internships;
- moving for a new job;
- attending weddings or funerals;
- returning to campus;
- tourism;
- business travel.

The historical change is not captured by saying only that "people traveled less."

The deeper change is that **future mobility became less predictable**.

## The ordinary travel radius can shrink without physical infrastructure disappearing

Railways, airports and highways still existed.

The trains had not been uninvented.

Yet the practical radius of ordinary life could shrink sharply because a transport system is not only tracks and vehicles. It also depends on the ability to use them predictably.

This is exactly the same principle the repository applies to older technology:

> existence is not equivalent to ordinary availability.

## Long-distance relationships changed character

Before smartphones, long-distance family life was constrained by communication cost.

By 2020, communication was cheap and continuous:

- video calls;
- group chats;
- photo sharing;
- mobile payment;
- online shopping for relatives.

This meant people could remain socially present while physically separated.

But digital contact was not equivalent to:

- eating together;
- helping with childcare;
- accompanying an elderly parent;
- moving furniture;
- visiting someone in hospital;
- attending a family ritual.

The pandemic therefore made the distinction between **information proximity** and **physical proximity** unusually visible.

## Travel planning developed backup logic

A household could increasingly plan with two versions:

**Plan A:** go in person.

**Plan B:** postpone / cancel / join remotely / send money / send goods / call by video.

This habit of contingency is an important part of pandemic life.

## 2022: mobility remained unusually compressed

In 2022, total passenger traffic fell to 5.59 billion trips and railway traffic to 1.67 billion passenger trips.

Domestic tourism totaled 2.53 billion trips, down 22.1% from 2021.

Source:
https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202302/t20230227_1918979.html

Again, this does not mean every person lived the same way. Local conditions and occupations differed substantially.

But at national scale, movement was still far from the ordinary pre-pandemic pattern.

## 2023: distance becomes ordinary again very quickly

In 2023:

- railway passenger trips rose to 3.85 billion;
- total passenger traffic rose 66.5%;
- domestic tourism reached 4.89 billion trips, up 93.3% from 2022.

Source:
https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202402/t20240228_1947918.html

The speed of this rebound explains part of the "forgotten years" effect.

Once:

- weekend trips;
- business travel;
- campus movement;
- family visits;
- holidays

became ordinary again, the contingency habits of 2020–2022 could quickly feel like an unreal interruption.

## What future oral histories should ask

- Which trip did you cancel first in 2020?
- When did you first realize an already-booked trip might not happen?
- Did you live in a different city from your partner or parents?
- How often did you video-call instead of visiting?
- Did you postpone changing jobs because moving cities felt difficult?
- Did you choose a job nearer home afterward?
- When did you first travel again without thinking of a backup plan?
- Did 2023 feel like "returning to normal" immediately, or only gradually?

Those questions recover the lived meaning behind transport statistics.
