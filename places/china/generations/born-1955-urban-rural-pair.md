# Born c.1955: the same birth year in urban and rural China

This file compares two hypothetical people born around 1955.

It is not a claim that all urban or rural people lived alike. Its purpose is to show how the **same calendar age can sit inside different institutional worlds**.

## Age 20 — 1975

### Urban pathway

A young urban adult is more likely to encounter:

- formal work-unit employment or institution-linked work;
- stronger connection between job, residence and welfare;
- denser secondary-school and professional routes;
- smaller geographic labor radius than later generations;
- scarce but institutionally organized housing.

### Rural pathway

A rural young adult is more likely to encounter:

- household agricultural production;
- local nonfarm work or craft work where available;
- earlier integration into family labor;
- more limited educational access;
- marriage and household production closely connected.

The difference is not one of aspiration alone.

The available institutions differ.

---

## Age 22 — 1977

The reopening of the national university entrance examination creates a new educational route, but access remains extraordinarily scarce.

About **5.7 million people applied and 272,971 were admitted**, an admission rate of roughly 4.78%.

Source:

- https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/cndy/2015-03/31/content_19957679.htm

For an urban secondary-school graduate, the route may be more visible through school networks and local educational institutions.

For a rural young adult, the same reopening can be historically meaningful while still constrained by schooling history, household labor obligations and local preparation conditions.

The same new door does not imply equal distance to the door.

---

## Age 30 — 1985

### Urban household

By 1985 an urban household may increasingly experience early durable-goods expansion.

National urban household benchmarks include:

- washing machines: 48.29 per 100 households;
- refrigerators: 6.58;
- color TVs: 17.21;
- electric fans: 73.91.

Source:

- https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/Statisticaldata/yearlydata/YB1999e/j06e.htm

The household may still be constrained by space, but appliances are beginning to reorganize domestic labor and aspiration.

### Rural household

Rural durable ownership remains far lower at the same moment.

In 1985 per 100 rural households:

- washing machines: 1.90;
- refrigerators: 0.06;
- color TVs: 0.80;
- electric fans: 9.66.

Source:

- https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/Statisticaldata/yearlydata/YB2000e/J24E.htm

Thus two thirty-year-olds can both be raising families while inhabiting different technological eras.

---

## Housing at age 30

Historical residential floor-space series report, for 1985:

- urban: about **10.0 m² per person**;
- rural: about **14.7 m² per person**.

Source:

- https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/ndsj/2008/html/J0935e.htm

The larger rural figure should not be translated into simple greater comfort.

Rural housing may have:

- more floor space;
- different sanitation;
- different heating;
- different water access;
- different room functions;
- household production taking place at home.

Urban housing can be smaller but connected to denser infrastructure and work-unit services.

---

## Age 45 — 2000

By 2000 the two life courses have diverged further.

### Urban person

A midlife urban worker may be adapting to:

- changing employment security;
- housing privatization or home ownership;
- adult children's education;
- new consumer technologies;
- early Internet and mobile-phone systems.

### Rural person

A rural midlife adult may be experiencing:

- large-scale labor migration by children or younger relatives;
- household farming combined with wage income;
- remittances;
- rapid but still incomplete appliance adoption;
- greater geographic separation of family members.

Rural-to-urban migrant-worker stock rose from about **21.6 million in 1990 to 78.8 million in 2000**.

Source:

- https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099020325194013546/pdf/P180848-980daabd-c4df-4314-a796-5df3bf5c97c5.pdf

This means the rural person's children's future can become geographically different from their own.

---

## Age 60–65 — 2015–2020

The largest later-life divergence is retirement.

CHARLS 2018 shows retirement rates among ages 60–64 of approximately:

- urban: **70.8%**;
- rural: **26.1%**.

The same chronological age therefore does not produce the same working status.

See:

- [`../../../data/china-retirement-rates-charls-2018.csv`](../../../data/china-retirement-rates-charls-2018.csv)

An urban 63-year-old may experience retirement as a recognized stage.

A rural 63-year-old may still farm, perform casual labor or combine work with grandchild care.

---

## Grandparenthood

Both may provide childcare to grandchildren, but the structure can differ.

Research using CHARLS shows substantial grandchild-care participation in both urban and rural China, with rural care more often intertwined with labor migration and skipped-generation households.

Sources:

- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6244459/
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10435994/

Thus older age may involve:

> pension + grandchild care

for one person, and:

> continued work + grandchild care + dependence on children's transfers

for another.

---

## The core paired comparison

Born in the same year does not mean growing old in the same system.

For this cohort, a useful shorthand is:

### Urban c.1955

> **institutionally organized work → crowded but infrastructure-rich household improvement → housing/employment transition in midlife → pension-backed retirement more likely**

### Rural c.1955

> **household production → slower appliance/infrastructure adoption → children's migration becomes central → continued work and family-supported old age more likely**

The point is not to declare one path better.

It is to preserve the fact that two people born in 1955 can have different meanings for school, work, home, migration and age 60.
