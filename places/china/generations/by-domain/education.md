# Education across cohorts: from scarce access to mass differentiation to AI-assisted learning

Education changes not only because more people attend school.

It changes because the **question a family is trying to solve** changes.

---

## Cohort born c.1955 — education as reopened scarce opportunity

The 1977 national university entrance examination reached this cohort at about age 22.

Historical figures report roughly 5.7 million applicants and 272,971 admissions, an admission rate of 4.78%.

Source:

- https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/cndy/2015-03/31/content_19957679.htm

The relevant question is:

> **Can higher education become possible again for me at all?**

University is a rare route, not a routine family expectation.

## Cohort born c.1965 — education as a selective ladder

This cohort reaches adolescence after university admissions resume, but places remain scarce.

For many families, continuing education is valuable precisely because it can change occupational placement.

The problem is still largely one of **access**.

A strong student may aim at university or technical/professional schooling, while many peers enter work much earlier.

## Cohort born c.1975 — education expands while labor-market rules begin changing

This cohort reaches age 20 in 1995.

Higher education is expanding, but still far from a mass route.

At the same time, jobs become more marketized.

Education therefore begins to perform a new function:

> **not merely qualifying for a position, but improving one's position inside a changing labor market.**

Credentials increasingly travel with the worker rather than being useful only inside one assigned institution.

## Cohort born c.1985 — university becomes a normal family planning question

The Ministry of Education's gross higher-education enrollment rate rose from:

- 1990: 3.4%;
- 1995: 7.2%;
- 2000: 12.5%;
- 2004: 19.0%.

Source:

- https://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_sjzl/moe_560/moe_1389/moe_1390/moe_1393/201002/t20100226_20347.html

For a person born in 1985, age 20 arrives after mass expansion has begun.

The family question becomes:

- which university;
- which major;
- whether to continue to postgraduate study;
- how much education spending is justified.

Urban family research increasingly describes children's education as a major consumption and investment project.

Source:

- https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40711-019-0099-8

## Cohort born c.1995 — access gives way to differentiation

By this cohort's adulthood, university is common enough that “has a degree” alone may not resolve occupational differentiation.

The education problem becomes:

- institution level;
- major;
- internships;
- postgraduate degree;
- certificates;
- overseas experience where feasible;
- practical skill.

The question shifts from:

> **Can I enter higher education?**

into:

> **How do I make my education distinguish me?**

## Cohort born c.2005 — AI enters the learning process itself

This cohort reaches late secondary school and university after generative AI becomes widely visible.

The educational problem gains a new layer:

- what tasks can AI do;
- what must the student still understand;
- how to verify generated content;
- what counts as authorship;
- whether first drafts still need to begin from a blank page;
- whether coding competence means writing syntax or supervising generated code.

The Ministry of Education began formalizing AI education in primary/secondary schooling in 2024, and its 2026 AI + Education Action Plan calls for broader AI education and AI as a public foundational university course.

The future question may no longer be whether students use AI, but **what kind of learner AI produces**.

---

# The education problem changes in stages

A rough historical sequence:

> **access**
>
> → **selection**
>
> → **credential expansion**
>
> → **differentiation within mass education**
>
> → **AI-assisted learning and verification**

Each stage preserves older inequalities while adding new ones.

## Parents also change

A parent born in 1955 may have experienced scarce educational opportunity.

Their child born in the 1980s may receive concentrated family educational investment.

That child may later become a parent buying tutoring, technology, housing near schools or AI-enabled learning tools.

Education therefore becomes an increasingly long intergenerational financial project.

## What to preserve

Useful ordinary-life sources include:

- school timetables;
- tuition and fee records;
- entrance-exam materials;
- family letters about whether to continue school;
- tutoring receipts;
- first computer used for homework;
- early online-course screenshots;
- first AI-assisted assignment drafts;
- parent-child conversations about majors and careers.

These materials reveal the changing meaning of “study hard” better than enrollment totals alone.
