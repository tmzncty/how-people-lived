# Millennial-era ordinary life in China

This folder uses a **cohort-history** method rather than treating "Millennial" as a rigid imported demographic identity.

The anchor is people born roughly **1985–1995**, especially the useful thought experiment of someone born around **1990**.

The main sequence is:

- childhood in the late 1990s;
- adolescence in the PC-Internet 2000s;
- young adulthood during the smartphone/mobile-Internet transition of the early 2010s;
- early middle adulthood interrupted by the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Why this cohort is useful

A person born around 1990 can remember:

- television before streaming;
- landlines before personal mobile phones;
- Internet cafés before universal home broadband;
- QQ before always-on mobile social media;
- MP3 files before streaming libraries;
- paper tickets before fully app-based travel;
- cash-heavy daily life before mobile payment became ambient;
- online shopping becoming normal rather than pre-existing;
- the shift from being reachable by phone to being continuously reachable online.

This makes the cohort especially useful for reconstructing the **transition from scheduled and place-bound information to ambient digital life**.

## Key research rule

Do not turn this folder into a nostalgia gallery.

Every nostalgic object should answer:

1. What problem did it solve?
2. How expensive was it?
3. Who had access?
4. What older practice did it replace?
5. What newer practice replaced it?
6. How long was the transition period?
7. What did the object change about time, privacy, friendship, distance or boredom?

The goal is not to prove the past was better.

It is to recover the **texture of a technological transition that happened within one person's lifetime**.
