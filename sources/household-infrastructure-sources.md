# Household infrastructure source bundle

A working source list for the project's next expansion into lighting, electricity, water, sanitation, cooking fuel, communications and Victorian London.

This file supplements `source-catalog.md`. Inclusion means "useful lead / institutional source," not "all claims should be accepted without source criticism."

## United States electrification

### U.S. Census Bureau — historical dwelling electrification benchmarks

Useful for all-dwelling versus farm-dwelling electric service in 1920, 1930 and 1940.

https://www.census.gov/about/history/stories/monthly/2025/september-2025.html

### Smithsonian — rural electrification

Useful for the household consequences of lacking electricity and for explaining the economic problem of extending utility lines into low-density areas.

https://americanhistory.si.edu/explore/stories/power-people-rural-electrification-brought-more-lights

### Smithsonian — early household wiring

Useful for the difference between "house connected to electricity" and "modern electrical household": costly retrofits, limited sockets, combination gas/electric fixtures and early appliance connection through lamp sockets.

https://americanhistory.si.edu/lighting/history/blotters/blot172.htm

### Smithsonian — 16 Elm Street

Documented household case showing electricity, coal/wood cooking, oil cooking, icebox use and delayed indoor sanitation coexisting across decades.

https://www.americanhistory.si.edu/ne/node/42052

## Lighting

### English Heritage — history of lighting

Useful for gas lighting in British homes, especially middle-class adoption and the heat, smell, dirt and safety costs of gas.

https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/inspire-me/blog/blog-posts/history-of-lighting/

### Science and Industry Museum — electric lighting at John Rylands Library

Useful for the transition from gas to electric lighting in late-nineteenth-century commercial/public buildings and motives involving smoke, fumes and fire risk.

https://blog.scienceandindustrymuseum.org.uk/five-vintage-light-bulbs/

## Water and sanitation

### The National Archives — Victorian homes

Useful introductory contrast between poor urban housing with shared pumps/toilets and wealthier housing with newer domestic services.

https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/victorian-homes/

### English Heritage — Victorian daily life

Useful for the gradual extension of clean water, drains and gas into more prosperous working-class housing in the late nineteenth century.

https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/learn/story-of-england/victorian/daily-life/

### Science Museum — Victorian water closet and sewer context

Useful for household sanitation technology, the 1848 Public Health Act, shared facilities and London's nineteenth-century sewer transition.

https://blog.sciencemuseum.org.uk/adopt-an-object-trail/

### Science Museum — Bazalgette and London sewerage

Useful for municipal sewer construction and the relationship between domestic waste systems, the Thames and public health.

https://blog.sciencemuseum.org.uk/joseph-bazalgette-londons-sewer-king/

### English Heritage — Audley End domestic technology

Particularly useful as an **elite early-adopter case**: pumped/piped water, water closets, service systems and heating that existed long before mass adoption.

https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/audley-end-house-and-gardens/history-and-stories/country-house-technology/

## Cooking and household energy

### National Park Service — Kennedy household kitchen

A useful documented 1910s mixed-technology kitchen with electricity, hot/cold running water and a coal-or-gas stove.

https://www.nps.gov/places/jofi-kitchen.htm

### National Park Service — icebox and stove

Useful for the same household's icebox and coal/gas cooking context.

https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/the-icebox-the-predecessor-of-modern-refrigeration.htm

### Science Museum — Kitchen Spectacular

Useful for Alexis Soyer's unusually advanced 1842 Reform Club kitchen and the distinction between elite/institutional early adoption and ordinary domestic use.

https://blog.sciencemuseum.org.uk/kitchen-spectacular/

### English Heritage — Victorian food and railway distribution

Useful for connecting household food to nineteenth-century railway distribution and growing urban markets.

https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/learn/story-of-england/victorian/food-and-health/

## Post and telegraph

### The Postal Museum — Sending and Delivering

Useful for Victorian postal volume, very frequent urban postbox collections, possible same-day delivery and the late extension of universal home delivery.

https://www.postalmuseum.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/3-Sending-and-Delivering.pdf

### The Postal Museum — Search Room Guide

Useful for Penny Post background and the growth of annual inland letter traffic to more than 860 million by 1870.

https://www.postalmuseum.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Search-Room-Guide.pdf

### The Postal Museum — historical Post Office roles

Useful for telegraph messengers and the Post Office takeover of the telegraph monopoly in 1870.

https://staging1.postalmuseum.org/collections/the-archive/family-history/roles/

### The Postal Museum — pneumatic railway

Useful for the experimental underground mail connection between sorting offices and railway stations in nineteenth-century London.

https://staging1.postalmuseum.org/blog/the-pneumatic-railway/

## London transport

### London Transport Museum — first regular horse bus

Useful baseline: first regular service in 1829, Paddington to Bank, five miles in about an hour; its initial one-shilling fare was too expensive for an average worker.

https://library.ltmuseum.co.uk/portal/Default/en-GB/RecordView/Index/109

### London Transport Museum — first Underground railway

Useful baseline for Metropolitan Railway opening in 1863 between Paddington and Farringdon and its use of steam traction.

https://library.ltmuseum.co.uk/portal/Default/en-GB/RecordView/Index/108

## Research principle illustrated by this bundle

These sources repeatedly show that a household can contain several technological periods at once.

Examples include:

- electric light + coal stove;
- piped water + incomplete sanitation;
- gas cooking + icebox;
- city telegraph + no household telephone;
- municipal sewer + rented room without modern bathroom;
- mature electrical technology + unelectrified farm.

That coexistence is often more historically useful than a clean invention timeline.
