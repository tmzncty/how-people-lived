# Generational life-course source catalog

This catalog collects reusable sources for comparing Chinese birth cohorts without reducing generations to stereotypes.

The key question is always:

> **What did this cohort encounter at a particular age?**

## Method and value change

### Wu & Sun (2026) — work, family and leisure values, 1990–2018

Open-access article using six Chinese World Values Survey waves and age-period-cohort models.

Why it matters:

- work centrality declines over time;
- family and leisure centrality strengthen;
- period effects are more important than simple cohort effects in the models;
- useful warning against writing broad social change as “young generation personality.”

https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40711-026-00262-6

## Education

### 1977–79 university entrance examination historical review

Reports about 5.7 million applicants and 272,971 admissions in 1977.

Useful for showing how selective higher education remained when people born in the mid-1950s reached young adulthood.

https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/cndy/2015-03/31/content_19957679.htm

### Ministry of Education — higher-education gross enrollment

Historical series used in this repository:

- 1990: 3.4%
- 1995: 7.2%
- 2000: 12.5%
- 2004: 19.0%

Useful for comparing whether university was a rare route, realistic aspiration or normal family-planning question.

https://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_sjzl/moe_560/moe_1389/moe_1390/moe_1393/201002/t20100226_20347.html

### Educational mobility for the 1986–95 birth cohort (2024)

Uses 1982–2020 census educational distributions plus CGSS/CFPS parent-child data.

Key uses:

- women in the 1986–95 cohort reach a new relative educational position compared with men;
- educational mobility remains structured by urban/rural origin;
- useful exact benchmark: father–daughter rank correlation about 0.51 for urban-hukou origin versus 0.27 for rural-hukou origin in the 1986–95 cohort;
- the article explicitly cautions that some cross-cohort coefficient changes are statistically insignificant.

https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11113-024-09887-2

Dataset:

- `data/china-educational-mobility-1986-1995-selected.csv`

### Intergenerational educational mobility by birth cohort (2025)

Uses CGSS survey waves and birth-year groupings.

Useful for examining how educational mobility differs across cohorts rather than assuming education expansion affected every generation in the same way.

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0738059325001956

### Three-generation educational mobility

Nationally representative estimates linking grandparents, parents and children.

Useful for showing that a child's educational trajectory can remain connected to family resources across more than two generations.

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1043951X23000755

### Parent input and university access

Finds that parental education, communication and child trust are significantly associated with university entry in the context of higher-education expansion.

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1043951X22000190

## Employment and job mobility

### Knight & Yueh (2004) — job mobility of residents and migrants

Useful historical summary of the older urban labor system:

- administrative job allocation;
- restricted mobility;
- first job often long lasting;
- close work-unit relationship;
- 1990s erosion of lifetime employment and increased mobility.

The 1999 survey analyzed in the article reports average tenure of about:

- 19.9 years for urban residents;
- 4.5 years for rural–urban migrants.

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0147596704000551

Dataset:

- `data/china-job-tenure-urban-resident-migrant-1999.csv`

### Weak ties, strong ties and job mobility, 1978–2008

Uses surveys from 1998, 2004 and 2008 to trace job-search networks.

Useful for showing the shift from a closed allocation system toward active labor-market search.

The article reports jobs found through non-kin ties rising from less than 5% in 1978 to roughly 25% by 2008.

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378873315000726

### State-sector transition by labor-entry cohort

Life-course/cohort study asking whether the timing of labor-market entry changed exposure to state/private employment.

Useful for explaining why two people with similar occupations can experience market reform differently depending on when their careers began.

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1040260818300091

### School-to-work transition, CGSS 2003–2021

Constructs education / employment / neither sequences from ages 10–29 using retrospective information.

Useful for studying the emergence of a distinct period between finishing school and stable employment.

https://doi.org/10.1080/21620555.2025.2480280

## Women, employment, childbirth and migration

### Urban women's early-adulthood employment trajectories

Uses CGSS 2008 retrospective work histories to compare women born in the 1940s–1970s across ages 20–35.

Useful for avoiding male-default cohort histories and for examining how marriage/fertility interact with employment differently across periods.

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29108599/

### Women's long-run employment after first childbirth

CHARLS life-history sequence analysis covering non-agricultural women born in the 1940s–1970s.

Six reported trajectory types include long-term regular employment, early/late return, self-employment, long-term non-employment and informal employment.

https://www.society.shu.edu.cn/EN/Y2023/V43/I5/167

### Migration histories by gender and cohort, 1930–1969 births

Finds clear cohort and sex differences in migration. Education and non-agricultural employment raise migration chances; marriage and more births reduce migration, with stronger effects on women.

https://rkyj.ruc.edu.cn/CN/abstract/abstract3729.shtml

## Housing

### NBS historical residential floor-space series

Selected per-capita residential floor-space figures:

- 1978 urban 6.7 m² / rural 8.1 m²;
- 1985 urban 10.0 / rural 14.7;
- 1995 urban 16.3 / rural 21.0;
- 2005 urban 26.1 / rural 29.7.

Important: this later historical table reports residential-building floor space, which should not be silently mixed with older “net living space” measures.

https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/ndsj/2008/html/J0935e.htm

### Urban housing tenure change, 1988–2019

CHIP/CHFS-based study.

Key historical benchmarks:

- homeownership 13.5% in 1988;
- around 42% in 1995;
- near 80% by 2002;
- early increases heavily shaped by privatization of public housing.

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11771467/

### Housing reform, life course and first-home acquisition, Guangzhou 1980–2010

Event-history study of first property-right acquisition.

Useful for showing that first-home ownership has both period and cohort effects and that one “first home” can be produced by very different housing systems.

https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40711-019-0101-5

### Parental housing transfer to 1980–1996 birth cohorts

Uses the 2011–2013 China Household Finance Survey.

Identifies three broad transfer forms:

- no substantial parental help;
- partial help with mortgage down payment;
- full homeownership transfer.

https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/parental-love-on-time-strength-and-timing-in-homeownership-transf/

### Parental support for homeownership, six Chinese cities, 2000–2017 purchases

Reports parental financial support as consistently common and examines its relation to co-residence.

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264275123001221

### Housing prices and marriage delay (2026)

Using census and city-level evidence, estimates a 10% housing-price increase delays first marriage by about 0.89 months for urban-hukou holders in the 2006–2010 sample.

Useful for linking a material market to the timing of an adult milestone.

https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11146-026-10053-y

### Shanghai housing careers by origin

Sequence-based housing-career analysis showing locals, permanent migrants and temporary migrants entering ownership through different trajectories.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-03793-w

## Adult milestones and family formation

### Transition to adulthood in China, Germany and the US

Compares leaving home, first marriage and parenthood for cohorts born 1933–1988.

Useful for the age-30 comparison: in the Chinese sample, most people had married and become parents by age 30 even while education/employment pathways and urban/rural differences changed.

https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2057150X19838728

### First marriage in China, 1966–2008

Uses birth cohorts and education/hukou status to study gendered transition into first marriage.

Useful for comparing prime marriage-age cohorts before and during tertiary-education expansion.

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6240985/

### Partnership and parenthood among millennial young adults

Life-course study of partnership and parenthood in contemporary China.

https://www.demographic-research.org/articles/volume/43/6

## Family and children

### “Purchasing hope” — children's education in urban China

Useful for:

- education as family consumption;
- children born after 1980 as an early urban child-consumer cohort in the market economy;
- high parental investment;
- class differences in supplemental education.

https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40711-019-0099-8

### Only child and girls' educational opportunity, Wuhan 1998–99

Survey of 1,040 eighth graders.

Useful for comparing single-girl and single-boy families and showing similarly high urban educational aspirations.

https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0891243202016001005

### Life-course and cohort effects on parents' investment in children

Uses the 2017 Chinese Household Finance Survey to separate parent-age/life-course effects from cohort effects in educational investment.

https://www.demographic-research.org/articles/volume/43/8

### Children of reform and opening-up

Broad generational review of post-1980 and post-1990 youth, family structure and living standards.

Useful as a synthesis, but should not replace narrower primary statistics.

https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40711-020-00130-x

## Migration

### World Bank — rural-to-urban migrant worker stock

Selected estimates:

- 1990: 21.6 million;
- 2000: 78.8 million;
- 2008: 140.4 million;
- 2019: 174.3 million;
- 2022: 171.9 million.

Useful for showing when “go out to work” becomes a mass life route for rural youth.

https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099020325194013546/pdf/P180848-980daabd-c4df-4314-a796-5df3bf5c97c5.pdf

### Spatial and career mobility of urban/rural labor

CLDS life-history research comparing career mobility from first to current job and spatial migration across cohorts and origins.

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5679455/

## What still needs collecting

High-value missing sources include:

- oral histories grouped by birth cohort;
- rural women's cohort life histories;
- household budgets around first marriage and first child;
- first-job duration **by birth cohort**;
- first-home purchase age and parental support **by cohort**;
- retirement expectations by cohort;
- time-use data by age and period;
- first Internet / first mobile / first smartphone memories;
- work-satisfaction and job-change motives by age;
- AI adoption by occupation and birth cohort;
- diaries, letters, school essays and family photographs that show how people themselves described the future.

## Preservation rule

This repository should generally preserve:

- structured extracts;
- metadata;
- links;
- short quotations where necessary;
- summaries of evidence.

Do not copy modern copyrighted books, articles or proprietary datasets wholesale merely because they are historically useful.
