# Generational life-course source catalog

This catalog collects reusable sources for comparing Chinese birth cohorts without reducing generations to stereotypes.

The key question is always:

> **What did this cohort encounter at a particular age?**

## Method and value change

### Wu & Sun (2026) — work, family and leisure values, 1990–2018

Open-access article using six Chinese World Values Survey waves and age-period-cohort models.

Why it matters:

- work centrality declines over time;
- family and leisure centrality strengthen;
- period effects are more important than simple cohort effects in the models;
- useful warning against writing broad social change as “young generation personality.”

https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40711-026-00262-6

## Education

### 1977–79 university entrance examination historical review

Reports about 5.7 million applicants and 272,971 admissions in 1977.

Useful for showing how selective higher education remained when people born in the mid-1950s reached young adulthood.

https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/cndy/2015-03/31/content_19957679.htm

### Ministry of Education — higher-education gross enrollment

Historical series used in this repository:

- 1990: 3.4%
- 1995: 7.2%
- 2000: 12.5%
- 2004: 19.0%

Useful for comparing whether university was a rare route, realistic aspiration or normal family-planning question.

https://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_sjzl/moe_560/moe_1389/moe_1390/moe_1393/201002/t20100226_20347.html

### Intergenerational educational mobility by birth cohort (2025)

Uses CGSS survey waves and birth-year groupings.

Useful for examining how educational mobility differs across cohorts rather than assuming education expansion affected every generation in the same way.

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0738059325001956

## Employment and job mobility

### Knight & Yueh (2004) — job mobility of residents and migrants

Useful historical summary of the older urban labor system:

- administrative job allocation;
- restricted mobility;
- first job often last job;
- close work-unit relationship;
- 1990s erosion of lifetime employment and increased mobility.

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0147596704000551

### Weak ties, strong ties and job mobility, 1978–2008

Uses surveys from 1998, 2004 and 2008 to trace job-search networks.

Useful for showing the shift from a closed allocation system toward active labor-market search.

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378873315000726

## Housing

### NBS historical residential floor-space series

Selected per-capita residential floor-space figures:

- 1978 urban 6.7 m² / rural 8.1 m²;
- 1985 urban 10.0 / rural 14.7;
- 1995 urban 16.3 / rural 21.0;
- 2005 urban 26.1 / rural 29.7.

Important: this later historical table reports residential-building floor space, which should not be silently mixed with older “net living space” measures.

https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/ndsj/2008/html/J0935e.htm

### Urban housing tenure change, 1988–2019

CHIP/CHFS-based study.

Key historical benchmarks:

- homeownership 13.5% in 1988;
- around 42% in 1995;
- near 80% by 2002;
- early increases heavily shaped by privatization of public housing.

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11771467/

## Family and children

### “Purchasing hope” — children's education in urban China

Useful for:

- education as family consumption;
- children born after 1980 as the first urban child-consumer cohort in the market economy;
- high parental investment;
- class differences in supplemental education.

https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40711-019-0099-8

### Only child and girls' educational opportunity, Wuhan 1998–99

Survey of 1,040 eighth graders.

Useful for comparing single-girl and single-boy families and showing similarly high urban educational aspirations.

https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0891243202016001005

### Children of reform and opening-up

Broad generational review of post-1980 and post-1990 youth, family structure and living standards.

Useful as a synthesis, but should not replace narrower primary statistics.

https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40711-020-00130-x

## Migration

### World Bank — rural-to-urban migrant worker stock

Selected estimates:

- 1990: 21.6 million;
- 2000: 78.8 million;
- 2008: 140.4 million;
- 2019: 174.3 million;
- 2022: 171.9 million.

Useful for showing when “go out to work” becomes a mass life route for rural youth.

https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099020325194013546/pdf/P180848-980daabd-c4df-4314-a796-5df3bf5c97c5.pdf

## What still needs collecting

High-value missing sources include:

- oral histories grouped by birth cohort;
- household budgets around first marriage and first child;
- first-job duration by birth year;
- first-home purchase age and parental support by cohort;
- retirement expectations by cohort;
- time-use data by age and period;
- first Internet / first mobile / first smartphone memories;
- work-satisfaction and job-change motives by age;
- AI adoption by occupation and birth cohort;
- diaries, letters, school essays and family photographs that show how people themselves described the future.

## Preservation rule

This repository should generally preserve:

- structured extracts;
- metadata;
- links;
- short quotations where necessary;
- summaries of evidence.

Do not copy modern copyrighted books, articles or proprietary datasets wholesale merely because they are historically useful.
