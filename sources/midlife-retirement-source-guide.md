# Midlife, care and retirement source guide

This guide supports the repository's expansion beyond youth and household formation.

The core questions are:

- What does midlife require a household to finance upward and downward?
- When can a person actually stop working?
- Which resources flow from parents to children and which later flow back?
- How do housing, pensions and care link generations after age 45?

## CHARLS — core later-life infrastructure

China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) is a nationally representative longitudinal survey of people aged 45+ and their spouses.

National baseline: 2011–2012, approximately 17,700 respondents.

Project site:

- https://charls.pku.edu.cn/

Cohort profile:

- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3937970/

Use CHARLS for:

- employment and retirement;
- pensions;
- health;
- household wealth;
- children and transfers;
- living arrangements;
- life-history sequences.

## Retirement patterns: urban/rural and gender

### Giles et al. — One country, two systems

National CHARLS analysis documenting sharp urban/rural differences in retirement.

Selected CHARLS 2018 retirement rates:

- age 60–64: urban 70.8%, rural 26.1%;
- age 65–69: urban 81.3%, rural 35.2%.

The study also reports much higher pension income among urban retirees than rural retirees.

Source:

- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10187591/

Repository dataset:

- `data/china-retirement-rates-charls-2018.csv`

### Retirement trajectories, 2026

CHARLS life-course sequence analysis identifies five transition patterns:

- not retired;
- early retirement;
- late retirement from agricultural employment;
- statutory retirement;
- late retirement from self-employment.

Useful for replacing a single retirement-date model with trajectory history.

Source:

- https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0347550

## Sandwich generation

### Chen & Zhou (2022)

CFPS 2018 sample of 1,477 sandwich-generation couples.

Finds that obligatory financial exchange with aging parents is associated with lower educational expenditure on young children than more independent exchange patterns.

Use for:

- competing intergenerational claims on household money;
- midlife as resource allocation;
- links between elder support and children's education.

Source:

- https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1568484922000120

### 4-2-2 family caregiving stress

Qualitative work on dual responsibility for older parents and children.

Useful as personal/lifeworld evidence rather than population prevalence.

Source:

- https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/10443894231183406

## Adult children supporting older parents

### Financial transfers and older parents

CHARLS 2013 study of transfers from non-co-resident adult children to people aged 45+.

Useful for documenting upward financial flows in later life.

Source:

- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30012123/

### Rural educational investment and reciprocity

Longitudinal rural-family study connecting children's education and later financial transfers to aging parents.

Useful for the long exchange loop:

> parents invest in education → adult child earns → adult child supports parents.

Source:

- https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/ageing-and-society/article/childrens-education-and-their-financial-transfers-to-ageing-parents-in-rural-china-mothers-and-fathers-strategic-advantages-in-enforcing-reciprocity/BCF34740B3A578CE71BE51B2C7435E25

### Urban adult-child transfers, 1999

Study of Family Life in Urban China.

Useful for sibling differences and transfers between adult children and elderly parents.

Source:

- https://www.cjs.shu.edu.cn/EN/10.1177/2057150X15624085

## Housing wealth in old age

### Housing wealth and upward transfers, CHARLS 2015/2018

Finds older parents' housing wealth associated with greater financial transfers from adult children.

Use cautiously: this is an association studied with fixed-effects methods, not proof that inheritance expectation is the only mechanism.

Source:

- https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1043951X2500029X

This source is useful because housing does not stop being historically relevant once a mortgage is paid.

At older ages, housing can be:

- residence;
- asset;
- inheritance;
- care bargaining resource;
- family anchor.

## What to collect next

### Time

- childcare hours;
- elder-care hours;
- travel time to parents;
- hospital accompaniment;
- grandparent childcare.

### Money

- upward transfers to parents;
- downward transfers to children;
- mortgage/debt after age 50;
- pension amount;
- health spending;
- education spending on children/grandchildren.

### Space

- co-residence;
- parents living nearby;
- children living far away;
- homeownership and inheritance expectations.

### Subjective horizon

- expected retirement age;
- whether retirement is considered desirable or affordable;
- what people want to do after stopping work;
- whether people expect children to support them;
- whether adults expect to support their children beyond age 25/30.

## Preservation rule

Retirement and care are sensitive family topics.

When preserving personal materials, remove or redact:

- health identifiers;
- detailed financial account information;
- addresses;
- names of non-consenting relatives;
- medical records unless explicitly public and appropriate.

The historical goal is to preserve the structure of everyday life, not expose a family's private vulnerabilities.
