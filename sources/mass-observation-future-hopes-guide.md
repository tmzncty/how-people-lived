# Mass Observation as a source for future hopes and life plans

Mass Observation is unusually valuable for this repository because it did not only observe what people did. It also asked ordinary people what they thought, feared, expected and wanted.

## Why it matters

Founded in Britain in 1937, Mass Observation aimed to create an "anthropology of ourselves." It used investigators, volunteer writers, diaries and open-ended directives to document everyday life, thought and feeling.

Official archive introduction:

https://massobs.org.uk/about-mass-observation/

For this repository, the crucial feature is the **directive**: respondents were asked open-ended questions rather than reduced to a few fixed statistical categories.

## Subjects relevant to life horizons

The University of Sussex description of the directive material notes questions on:

- class
- marriage
- money
- health
- education
- hopes and fears for the future

as well as practical everyday topics such as food, clothing, housing, transport and separation from loved ones.

Source:

https://www.sussex.ac.uk/library/speccoll/collection_descriptions/massobsdirectives.html

This combination is ideal for *How People Lived* because it links material conditions to subjective expectations.

## The 1944 Family Survey

The Mass-Observation topic collection for Family Planning, 1944–49 describes a 1944 family survey of roughly 1,000 interviews.

The archive catalogue says the questionnaire included:

- work
- future hopes and plans
- hoped-for and actual number of children
- hopes for marriage among unmarried respondents
- attitudes toward career
- expectations for life after the war
- housing
- age
- family size
- reasons for having children

Catalogue:

https://www.sussex.ac.uk/library/speccoll/collection_catalogues/tclists/tc3.html

This is extraordinarily close to the project's life-horizon template.

## Questions this material could answer

### Marriage

- Did unmarried people assume they would marry?
- What conditions did they think necessary first?
- How did housing affect marriage plans?

### Children

- How many children did people want?
- Why?
- How did desired family size relate to income, housing and career?

### Career

- Did respondents describe work as a career, job, duty or temporary stage?
- Did marriage alter career expectations?

### Housing

- What did people imagine a satisfactory home would look like?
- Was independent housing part of adulthood?

### The future

- What did respondents expect life to become in five or ten years?
- Which material improvements were anticipated?
- Which anxieties dominated future planning?

## Methodological advantage

A household survey might tell us how many rooms a family had.

A directive or interview can tell us whether they believed they needed another room before having another child.

That difference is central to the project:

**condition → interpretation → goal → decision**

## Important limitations

Mass Observation respondents were not a perfect representative sample of Britain.

Future entries should preserve:

- respondent age
- sex
- occupation
- residence
- household status
- date of response
- exact directive question

Do not turn one vivid diary into "what Britain thought."

## Future repository work

- locate digitized or accessible individual responses where permissions allow
- build a small coded dataset of future-oriented questions
- compare stated goals by age, occupation and household status
- connect housing expectations to actual housing statistics
- connect marriage and family plans to income and rent
- compare private diaries with answers written for directives

## Why this belongs here

The repository's material-history side asks:

> What could a household actually afford and use?

Mass Observation can help answer the complementary question:

> **What did that household hope would happen next?**
