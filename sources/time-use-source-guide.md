# Time-Use Source Guide

Time-use evidence is essential for reconstructing ordinary life because it measures the resource every household receives in the same nominal quantity: **24 hours per day**.

The difficult part is that surveys do not always define activities, populations or reporting methods in the same way.

This guide records how to use the project's main China time-use sources without creating false precision.

## 2018 National Time Use Survey

Primary source:

- National Bureau of Statistics, 2018 National Time Use Survey communiqué: https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202302/t20230203_1900224.html

Official interpretation/comparison with 2008:

- https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/sjjd/202302/t20230202_1896141.html

Coverage:

- 11 provinces/municipalities;
- 20,226 households;
- 48,580 people.

Useful measures include:

- average time across all respondents;
- activity participation rate;
- average time among participants;
- sex split;
- urban/rural split;
- selected age groups;
- primary activities plus Internet use as an accompanying activity.

### Important distinction

If 20% of people perform an activity for three hours, the **population average** and **participant average** answer different questions.

Do not mix them.

Example:

- household work population average describes the total daily burden distributed across the population;
- household-work participant average describes the time cost for people who actually performed it that day.

## Official 2008 → 2018 comparisons

The NBS itself provides explicit comparison statements for selected categories.

These are safer to use than attempting to reconstruct a full harmonized 2008 dataset from scattered historical tables.

Officially reported changes include:

- physiological necessities +19 minutes;
- sleep/rest +17 minutes;
- total paid labor -4 minutes;
- household production/business -32 minutes;
- employment work +28 minutes;
- household work -17 minutes;
- accompanying/caring for family +30 minutes;
- transportation 75 → 38 minutes;
- Internet use 14 minutes → 2h42m.

Source:

- https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/sjjd/202302/t20230202_1896141.html

## 2024 Third National Time Use Survey

Main communiqué:

- https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202410/t20241031_1957216.html

Breakdowns by sex, urban/rural and broad age group:

- https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202411/t20241115_1957437.html

Method/source overview:

- https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202411/t20241115_1957435.html

The fieldwork took place May 11–31, 2024.

Respondents recorded:

- one weekday;
- one weekend day;
- activities in 15-minute intervals.

## Why 2024 is not a simple continuation of 2018

The third survey changed important design elements.

The NBS states that 2024:

- expanded coverage to 31 provinces/regions/municipalities plus XPCC;
- included ages 6–14 for the first time;
- expanded activity categories to 34;
- used a mobile mini-program for self-reporting as a new main collection method;
- adjusted the content and survey approach.

Source:

- https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202410/t20241031_1957216.html

Therefore:

> **Do not subtract a 2018 value from a 2024 value and automatically call the difference a social trend.**

Some high-level structural contrasts are informative, but precise trend analysis requires harmonized definitions.

## ICT use is not another 24-hour category

In both modern time-use releases, Internet/ICT use can accompany another activity.

Examples:

- messaging while commuting;
- watching video while eating;
- checking work messages while supervising a child;
- listening to an online course while doing another task.

Therefore ICT minutes can overlap with primary activity minutes.

Do not add ICT use to paid labor + unpaid labor + transport + leisure as if the day contained more than 24 hours.

This overlap is itself historically meaningful.

It marks a world in which information activity increasingly occupies **layers of time rather than exclusive blocks of time**.

## Time-use and gender

Time-use surveys are especially valuable because money statistics often miss unpaid work.

Useful questions include:

- total unpaid labor;
- household work;
- childcare;
- educational supervision;
- adult care;
- sex participation rates;
- participant hours.

The project should not infer that increased female employment means household labor automatically became equal.

## Time-use and cohort history

Cross-sectional time-use surveys measure people of different ages in one period.

They do not directly show how the same cohort changed over its life.

To build cohort history, combine:

1. time-use cross sections;
2. birth-cohort life-history data;
3. household composition;
4. work status;
5. presence/age of children;
6. elder-care responsibilities.

A 30-year-old in 2018 and a 30-year-old in 2024 are different cohorts observed under different systems.

## High-value future work

- obtain detailed public tables by age × sex where possible;
- separate employed/non-employed time budgets;
- reconstruct parent time by age of youngest child;
- compare commuting by city size;
- quantify grandparent childcare time rather than participation alone;
- connect time-use to household appliances and purchased services;
- connect remote work to commuting and unpaid labor;
- investigate whether AI reduces task time or raises expected output volume.

## Core preservation rule

Every time-use number should retain:

- survey year;
- geographic coverage;
- population definition;
- activity definition;
- average vs participant-average distinction;
- participation rate where available;
- whether the activity can overlap other activities;
- source URL.

Time is easy to measure badly because minutes look deceptively comparable.
