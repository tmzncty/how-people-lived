# Life Horizons Expansion Plan

This companion roadmap tracks the repository's second major axis: not only material life, but the futures people believed were available to them.

## Core questions

For each place, period and population, investigate:

- what counted as adulthood
- expected age of entry into work
- expected relationship between work and identity
- marriage expectations
- household-formation threshold
- children's role in family strategy
- education as investment
- debt and respectability
- savings targets
- home ownership or tenancy expectations
- old-age support
- retirement, if meaningful
- what counted as success
- what counted as failure
- what futures were realistic
- what futures were visible but unlikely
- what modern-looking options were effectively absent
- whether a major historical break changed these answers during the same person's adulthood
- whether more individual responsibility actually changed the sequence of adulthood
- which risks moved from institutions to households

## Historical-break method

Where possible, follow one birth cohort across at least three dates rather than comparing unrelated people.

Example:

> born 1920 → age 15 in 1935 → age 24 in 1944 → age 35 in 1955

Track education route, first-job expectation, geographic mobility, marriage timing, housing threshold, family-size plans, home ownership or allocation, consumer expectations, old-age expectations, main insecurity and confidence that life will improve.

For China and other highly heterogeneous settings:

> **Never let the cohort method erase place, class or rural/urban divergence.**

For fast peacetime transitions, also compare adjacent generations:

> parent born c. 1955 enters adulthood in the late 1970s / early 1980s
>
> child born c. 1980 enters adulthood around 2000–2005

Then ask which components changed from institutionally bundled to separately planned.

## Sequence check

Every modernization or individualization claim should now pass an additional test:

> **Did the timing or order of life milestones actually change?**

The China 1982–2005 evidence shows why this matters. School and labor-force entry shifted later, but marriage and parenthood moved much less, and the sequence remained ordered.

A society can therefore have:

- more education
- more job choice
- more market housing
- more migration
- more individual financing

without abandoning a strong expected order of adulthood.

This distinction should be checked in every country rather than assumed.

## Data opportunities

Life goals are qualitative, but many constraints can be made machine-readable:

- school-leaving age
- marriage age
- labour-force entry age
- first stable job
- higher-education gross enrolment
- occupational wage ladders
- urban / rural household income
- Engel coefficients
- rent / wage ratios
- savings rates
- pension eligibility and coverage
- household formation age
- home ownership
- housing allocation
- parental transfers for housing
- household size
- intergenerational co-residence
- educational attainment
- displacement / migration history
- rural migrant-worker counts
- household durables and communication technology

The repository should not reduce a life horizon to numbers, but quantitative constraints help define the boundary of the possible.

> London clerk, age 22, 1895

> British worker, born 1920 — compare age 15 / 24 / 35

> Chinese urban student, born 1920 — compare 1935 / wartime / 1955

> Chinese parent entering adulthood in 1980 → child entering adulthood in 2005

and see linked panels:

### What surrounds you?

- wages
- rent
- transport
- food
- water
- lighting
- communication
- household technology

### What future can you realistically plan?

- education
- occupation
- promotion
- migration
- marriage
- independent household or housing allocation
- children
- savings
- old age
- unlikely alternatives

### Who pays for each future milestone?

- individual
- parents
- spouse
- employer
- state/social insurance
- extended family
- debt/credit

### Does the order change?

- school
- work
- housing
- marriage
- parenthood
- support to parents
- retirement

That combination — **material world + imagined future + historical break + financing + sequence** — is now the project's full model of ordinary life.
