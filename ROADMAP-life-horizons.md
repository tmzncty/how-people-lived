# Life Horizons Expansion Plan

This companion roadmap tracks the repository's second major axis: not only material life, but the futures people believed were available to them.

## Core questions

For each place, period and population, investigate:

- what counted as adulthood
- expected age of entry into work
- expected relationship between work and identity
- marriage expectations
- household-formation threshold
- children's role in family strategy
- education as investment
- debt and respectability
- savings targets
- home ownership or tenancy expectations
- old-age support
- retirement, if meaningful
- what counted as success
- what counted as failure
- what futures were realistic
- what futures were visible but unlikely
- what modern-looking options were effectively absent

## Data opportunities

Life goals are qualitative, but many constraints can be made machine-readable:

- school-leaving age
- marriage age
- labour-force entry age
- occupational wage ladders
- rent / wage ratios
- savings rates
- pension eligibility
- household formation age
- home ownership
- household size
- intergenerational co-residence
- educational attainment

The repository should not reduce a life horizon to numbers, but quantitative constraints help define the boundary of the possible.

## The long-term interface question

A future reader should be able to choose a profile such as:

> London clerk, age 22, 1895

and see two linked panels:

### What surrounds you?

- wages
- rent
- transport
- food
- water
- lighting
- communication
- household technology

### What future can you realistically plan?

- promotion
- marriage
- independent household
- children
- savings
- old age
- unlikely alternatives

That pairing — **material world + imagined future** — is the project's full model of ordinary life.
