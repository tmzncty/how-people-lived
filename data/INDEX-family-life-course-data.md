# Family Life-Course Data Index

This index collects machine-readable files related to partnership, household formation, intergenerational support, grandparent care and later life.

## Marriage

### `china-first-marriage-age-selected-1990-2021.csv`

Selected national mean-age-at-first-marriage benchmarks:

- 1990 men: 23.57;
- 1990 women: 22.02;
- 2021 men: 25.63;
- 2021 women: 23.89.

Source:

- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9516001/

Use as broad timing benchmarks only. Urban/rural, education and regional differences remain important.

## Older-parent co-residence

### `china-elderly-coresidence-with-adult-children-1982-2010.csv`

Selected census-based endpoints for people age 65+ living with adult children:

- 1982: 69.4%;
- 2010: 51.7%.

Source:

- https://www.demographic-research.org/volumes/vol41/48/41-48.pdf

Do not interpret declining co-residence as disappearing family support.

## Grandparent childcare

### `china-grandparent-care-urban-rural-charls-2011.csv`

CHARLS 2011–2012 analytic-sample benchmark:

- rural grandparents providing care: 42.69%;
- urban grandparents providing care: 48.01%.

Source:

- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6244459/

The file records participation, not care intensity.

## Retirement

### `china-retirement-rates-charls-2018.csv`

Useful urban/rural contrast for later-life work status.

Selected age 60–64 totals:

- urban retirement rate: 70.8%;
- rural retirement rate: 26.1%.

Source:

- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10187591/

## Household structure

### `china-household-size-1980-2005.csv`

Tracks shrinking average household size in urban and rural samples.

Useful for understanding:

- sibling availability;
- co-residence;
- concentration of parental investment;
- future elder-care sharing.

## Housing

### `china-housing-space-1978-2005.csv`

Per-capita residential floor-space benchmarks.

### `china-urban-homeownership-1988-2002.csv`

Selected urban ownership benchmarks useful for household-formation history.

## Parent-child resources

No single direct transfer series is complete yet.

Relevant quantitative scaffolds include:

- housing ownership;

- household size;
- old-age support expectations;
- job tenure;

## Rule

Family data is especially easy to over-interpret.

A declining mean marriage age, co-residence rate or household size does **not** by itself reveal motive.

Use quantitative series to establish the changing boundary of plausible life arrangements, then combine them with surveys, interviews and life histories.
