# Pandemic Everyday-Life Index

This index collects the repository's materials on the COVID-19 years as ordinary-life history.

The focus is not political controversy. It is the practical reorganization of school, work, home, mobility, relationships, savings and future planning.

## Questions this archive should eventually answer

### Home

- How many people were simultaneously working or studying in one dwelling?
- How did room count, devices, internet and family schedules change experience?
- Which household objects became temporary institutional infrastructure?

### School

- What did an online school day actually look like?
- How were classes, exams, homework, internships and graduation reorganized?
- How did rural/urban digital access differ?

### Work

- Who could work remotely?
- Who stopped working?
- How did first-time remote work change attitudes toward commuting and work-life boundaries?
- Which workers discovered that their jobs could not be moved online at all?

### Food and household logistics

- How did shopping frequency change?
- Which forms of delivery or online retail became essential?
- Did households change storage, cooking or emergency reserves?

### Mobility

- How far did an ordinary person's practical travel radius shrink?
- Which visits were cancelled first?
- How did long-distance couples and families compensate digitally?
- When did travel feel ordinary again?

### Relationships and life plans

- Which weddings, moves, pregnancies, exams or job changes were postponed?
- Did living with parents again alter adult independence?
- Did distance from aging parents begin to matter more in later city/job choices?

### Money

- Did households build larger cash buffers?
- Which expenses became newly uncertain?
- Did job stability become more valuable after a sudden interruption in normal work?

### Memory

- Which routines disappeared almost immediately in 2023?
- Which habits remained?
- Which artifacts survive only in old phones and accounts?
- What does a person remember incorrectly because 2020–2022 now blur together?

## Core historical insight

The pandemic did not only interrupt activities.

It changed a deeper assumption:

> **ordinary life could no longer be scheduled as if the next week were guaranteed to resemble the current one.**

That is why the period belongs in a life-horizon history, not only in medical or political history.
