# Life Horizon Template

Use this template when the main question is not only how someone lived, but what they thought a life should become.

## Immediate priorities

What mattered this month or year?

- income
- food
- housing
- employment
- marriage
- education
- health
- family obligations
- debt
- migration
- other

## Expected sequence of life

What sequence was considered normal or desirable?

Example structure:

> school → work → marriage → household formation → children → children's establishment → old age

Do not assume this sequence. Reconstruct it from evidence.

## What counted as success?

Separate:

### Socially praised

What did institutions and communities describe as admirable?

### Commonly pursued

What did people actually invest time and money in?

### Personally desired

What do individual testimonies say people wanted?

## What counted as failure or misfortune?

- unemployment
- debt
- dependence
- inability to marry
- poor health
- loss of housing
- family breakdown
- children failing to establish themselves
- loss of reputation
- other

## Outside the horizon

Which modern-looking options were effectively absent, inaccessible, or not yet a recognized life path?

This section is especially important for preventing presentism.

## Family expectations

What did parents, spouse, children or wider kin expect?

## Peer and community expectations

What did neighbours, colleagues, classmates, religious communities or professional groups reward or condemn?

## Age thresholds

When was someone considered:

- old enough to work?
- an adult?
- marriageable?
- "late" to marry?
- established?
- old?

Use evidence rather than modern thresholds.

## Children and intergenerational goals

Did people seek a better life mainly for themselves, or for their children?

Questions:

- schooling
- apprenticeship
- inheritance
- property
- occupation
- migration
- marriage prospects

## Old age

What was the expected source of support?

- continued work
- savings
- pension
- property
- children
- charity
- community / religious support
- institutional care

## Direct testimony

Record brief, legally usable quotations only where wording matters. Otherwise summarize.

What actions reveal priorities?

Examples:

- buying insurance
- delaying marriage
- taking examinations
- migration
- evening study
- household purchase sequences
- children's schooling decisions
