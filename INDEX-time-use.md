# Time-Use and the 24-Hour Life Index

This index treats **time itself as historical infrastructure**.

A life is not only a sequence of milestones such as school, work, marriage, housing and retirement. It is also a recurring 24-hour budget.

The central question is:

> **Who or what occupies the hours of an ordinary day, and how does that change across generations?**

## Why time-use belongs in ordinary-life history

Material improvements often change **how labor is distributed**, not simply how much labor exists.

For example:

- washing machines reduce some forms of manual laundry labor;
- delivery services reduce some shopping/cooking logistics;
- higher education lengthens time in formal learning;
- longer commutes consume time outside paid work;
- childcare can become more education-intensive;
- smartphones and AI can move information work into moments that previously counted as waiting or leisure.

A historical household can therefore become wealthier while its members still feel time-poor.

## Key China anchor: 2018

The 2018 National Time Use Survey covered 48,580 people in 20,226 households across 11 provinces/municipalities.

Selected daily averages across all respondents:

- paid labor: 4h24m;
- unpaid labor: 2h42m;
- discretionary personal activity: 3h56m;
- transportation: 38m;
- Internet use (as a simultaneous/accompanying activity): 2h42m.

The gender difference in unpaid work was especially large:

- men: 1h32m;
- women: 3h48m.

For ages 25–34, average time spent accompanying/caring for children was:

- men: 29m;
- women: 1h55m.

Source:

- National Bureau of Statistics, 2018 National Time Use Survey: https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202302/t20230203_1900224.html

## The 2008 → 2018 household shift

The NBS comparison of the second survey with 2008 is historically useful.

Between 2008 and 2018:

- household-work time fell by 17 minutes per day;
- time accompanying/caring for family rose by 30 minutes;
- about 85% of family-care time in 2018 was spent on children's life and study;
- women's unpaid work remained far higher than men's despite a small narrowing of the gap;
- employment-work time among participants increased even as family-production work participation declined.

This suggests a major transformation:

> **some household labor moved from maintaining things toward maintaining and cultivating people.**

Source:

- NBS interpretation of the 2018 survey: https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/sjjd/202302/t20230202_1896141.html

### Comparison warning

Do **not** treat 2018 and 2024 as a perfectly harmonized time series.

The 2024 survey changed:

- geographic coverage;
- age coverage;
- activity classification;
- data-collection method;
- diary design and reporting framework.

The two surveys can support structural comparison, but precise trend claims should use only explicitly comparable categories or official NBS comparisons.

## Questions for cohort history

### Childhood

- How many hours are school, homework and commuting?
- How much unsupervised time exists?
- When does screen time become a large part of leisure?

### Young adulthood

- How much time is paid work versus commuting?
- Does postgraduate study postpone paid-work time?
- Does living with parents reduce household labor or increase family obligations?

### Parenthood

- How much time shifts from domestic maintenance to child cultivation?
- Who does the unpaid work?
- Does grandparent care substitute for parental time?

### Midlife

- How much time goes upward to aging parents and downward to children?
- How much discretionary time remains after work, commuting and care?

### Old age

- Does paid labor disappear?
- Is freed time converted into housework, grandchild care, exercise or leisure?
- Does urban/rural retirement divergence create completely different daily schedules after age 60?

## Core insight

A society can give people more appliances, higher incomes and faster information while still increasing the amount of **coordination, supervision and attention** required of an ordinary day.

So the repository should ask not only:

> What did people own?

but:

> **What did they have time for?**
